What this page shows

One session of functional MRI, run by run, in the anatomy. The page serves two kinds of quality control at once (Tomas Knapen, 2026-11-14): of the raw data, and of the preprocessing pipeline. Two streams of every run lie on the same slices, with the white and pial surfaces of FreeSurfer as contours:

  • raw: the raw BIDS series with nothing done to it but one rigid placement into the anatomy for the whole session (the stored registration of the session's anchor run). No motion, slice-timing or distortion correction, and no other processing. For the raw data it shows head motion within a run, artefacts confined to single volumes, distortion and dropout as acquired, and how far the head moved between runs.
  • preprocessed: the series as fMRIPrep stored it. For the pipeline it shows what remains after its corrections, and how the corrected series conforms to the contours.

The difference between the two streams, and the raw stream's misfit to the contours, are what the page exists to show: the effect of the preprocessing of that run, and the state of the raw data. The line under the run buttons says what the pipeline did to the run (head-motion correction; distortion correction and its method, or none; slice-timing correction or none), as fMRIPrep's summary report of the run states it, so that a difference can be read as the effect of those steps. Where distortion correction was applied, the raw stream shows the distortion as acquired against the contours, and the preprocessed stream how well the correction brought the series onto them. Brain within half a voxel of the edge of the raw field of view is black in the raw stream.

A run that is not the anchor is drawn through the anchor's registration, so the raw stream shows how far the head moved since the anchor run (raw data). How far that puts it from where its own stored registration would is written under the run buttons. A run beyond mm is flagged: that is a statement about the raw data under one registration (the head, or the subject, moved that much, or a stored registration failed), not a fault of the page.

Exact values. Every voxel on screen is the stored 8-bit value, one block per voxel, no smoothing. The stills (mean, SD) are computed before the 8 bits; the SD has a window of its own.

Difference is raw minus preprocessed of the two 8-bit values. Red to yellow: raw is higher. Blue to cyan: raw is lower. The colour saturates at the chosen scale, which is written under the views.

Markers. A volume whose framewise displacement exceeds the threshold has a band in the traces, and the frame carries a red marker that stays for at least ms however fast it plays. Volumes that the confounds table marks as non-steady-state have an amber band and marker.

Playing paints every volume; when the computer is slower than the rate, playing takes longer and nothing is skipped. The status line says how many volumes per second were painted. A long run is held in memory in pieces around the current volume, up to .
