# Decisions

Log of design decisions. Settled ones record why; open ones record what decides them. Add an entry whenever a choice would be hard to reverse.

## Settled

D1. Local FreeSurfer directories are a first-class source. The census is one use of the package, not its only one. Consequence: everything downstream of `Source` sees local paths only; `surfcensus review PATH` must work offline in one command.

D2. Default renderer is pure Python (nibabel + matplotlib), Freeview is optional. Reason: headless, deterministic, no FreeSurfer license, parallel. Freeview stays for figures and as a cross-check.

D3. Three planes around the occipital pole, ping-pong, with frame-level scrubbing. Reason: Tomas's specification; scrubbing because experienced raters work frame by frame.

D4. Cortical thickness is the quantitative anchor, scored per vertex in fsaverage space (fsaverage6 default) against a per-vertex reference distribution. Parcel means are kept as a cheap baseline. Reason: the failure is local, and normal thickness varies over short distances in occipital cortex, so only a location-matched reference can say what is abnormal; fsaverage also puts rater marks and metrics on the same vertices. Supersedes the earlier plan of per-subject direction-of-normal ROIs, which remain only as an optional cross-check.

D8. Raters mark errors by clicking, and marks are resolved to native and fsaverage6 vertices. Required for positive ratings in census mode, optional locally. Reason: turns per-subject ratings into spatial ground truth, which enables census error maps, vertex-level detection analysis, and a test of whether the metric flags the right place.

D10. Datasets are shown by pseudonym by default, in the app, gallery, figures, tables and released data. Reason: to keep goodwill with the labs that shared data; the paper's point is about practice in the field, not about individual groups. Implementation:

- The pseudonym is a keyed hash: the first 8 hex characters of HMAC-SHA256(key, "<source>:<accession>"), prefixed `D-` (e.g. `D-3f9a1c07`). A plain unkeyed hash would be worthless: OpenNeuro accessions are a small enumerable set, so anyone could hash all of them and invert the mapping in seconds.
- The key is 32 random bytes generated by `surfcensus key init`, stored outside the run directory and outside version control (default `~/.config/surfcensus/dataset.key`, path configurable). Run directories record only the key's fingerprint (SHA-256 of the key, first 12 hex), so outputs made with different keys cannot be mixed by accident.
- The catalog stores accessions (it must, to fetch data), but no exported artefact does. `surfcensus export` writes pseudonyms only; `surfcensus export --reveal` writes the mapping table and requires the key. Collisions among 8-hex pseudonyms are checked at discovery and resolved by lengthening the affected pseudonyms.
- Reviewers or editors who need the mapping get it confidentially.

What pseudonymization does not do: it is goodwill, not secrecy. Per-dataset subject counts, field strength, age ranges and the renders themselves are derived from public data and can, with effort, be matched to OpenNeuro metadata. Released per-dataset tables therefore report coarse descriptors (field strength, subject count binned) rather than exact acquisition details. The paper should state plainly that identities are withheld as a courtesy, not hidden.

D9. The per-vertex reference is built twice: first from all subjects, then from rater-passed subjects. Reason: common failures contaminate the reference exactly where they occur; reporting both measures that cost.

D5. OpenNeuroDerivatives and embedded derivatives are analysed separately. Reason: one pipeline versus heterogeneous pipelines answer different questions.

D6. Raters are blind to dataset, scores and other ratings during rating. Local mode may sort by score unless `--blind`.

D7. The frontend has no build step. Reason: pip-installable without Node; easy to maintain.

D11 (2026-11-12). Render output is per-panel slice tiles in two layers, composed in the browser: a lossy grayscale WebP of the T1 slice and a lossless RGBA WebP of the contours. The browser stacks the layers, places the three panels, draws crosshairs and orientation letters, and plays both playback modes from frame tables in `render.json`. `anim.mp4` and `poster.webp` remain composite three-panel strips. Reason: lossy WebP at quality 80 desaturates thin coloured lines (mean absolute error 47, 16, 70 of 255 on contour pixels, measured on `ds000102` sub-01); composite strips cost about 10 MB per subject against about 4 MB for layered tiles (estimate from 15 sagittal tiles, one hemisphere); a separate contour layer lets raters hide the contours to see the tissue under the line; and one pixel-to-voxel affine per panel replaces one per frame. Supersedes the composite-strip frames first described in `rendering.md`. Consequence for the protocol: raters can toggle contours, which the pilot should take into account when timing ratings.

D12 (2026-11-12). The occipital box is anchored on the geometric pole (most posterior pial vertex per hemisphere). From milestone 3 the renderer also maps a fixed fsaverage pole vertex to native space through `sphere.reg` and records its distance to the geometric pole in `render.json`. The pilot decides from those distances whether the anchor should change. First data (2026-11-13, `rendering.md`): 1.2 and 6.3 mm in the two hemispheres of one FreeSurfer 6.0.1 subject, 8.6 and 2.0 mm in one of 7.3.2. Reason: the geometric pole needs no template, so single-subject rendering stays self-contained, but it depends on head pitch and on the pial surface being correct at the pole.

D13 (2026-11-12). Panels follow Freeview's default 2D display orientation, with orientation letters on the panel edges, so the phase 0 comparison with Freeview is like for like. Implemented as two config switches (`radiological`, default true: subject's left on the right of the coronal and axial panels; `sagittal_anterior_left`, default false: posterior at the left). `render.json` records the orientation used. The defaults were first set from memory, with anterior at the left of the sagittal panel; reading Freeview's source the same day corrected that. In `RenderView2D.cpp` (dev branch; the sagittal and radiological cases are the same in v6.0.0 and v7.1.1) the sagittal camera sits at +x with view-up +z, which puts anterior at the right; the coronal camera sits at +y and the axial camera at −z with view-up +y, which is radiological with anterior up. A published Freeview screenshot shows the same edge labels. A neurological view exists only as an option (`-neuro-view`, from 7.4.0). The by-eye comparison of milestone 1 remains the check on a rendered frame.

D14 (2026-11-12). The fsaverage6 template (spheres, cortex label, search-region labels) is looked up in this order: a local FreeSurfer install, an `fsaverage/` directory next to the subjects, then a copy bundled in the wheel (about 1 to 2 MB). Reason: D1 requires `surfcensus review` to work offline, which fetch-on-first-use does not. Condition: bundling goes ahead only after Tomas confirms that the FreeSurfer licence permits redistributing these fsaverage-derived files (nilearn and TemplateFlow do so; the terms have not been read for this project).

D15 (2026-11-12; refined the same day in the review of milestone 1). Vertex-level spatial detectability is evaluated, per subject, only on search-region vertices that the subject's tiles show: the vertices with `visible` true in `vertex_map.npz`, whose pial position lies in the clickable region of `rendering.md`. Reason: raters can only mark what a tile shows, so search-region vertices that no tile shows would count as negatives that nobody inspected. `visible` is a property of the vertex's position, not of marks: a mark can resolve to a vertex that is not visible and still counts (`analysis_plan.md`, and D19, verification of the review fixes). The first wording was "inside that subject's rendered box (the vertices in `vertex_map.npz`)". It treated three sets as one: the box, what the tiles show (10 mm more than the box in x and z and 13.5 mm more in y with the default config), and the contents of the file, which now also holds a margin of vertices beyond what is shown, so that marks near a tile edge resolve to the right vertex. On `ds000102` sub-01 the box holds 31,548 pial vertices, 55,534 are visible, and the file holds 74,654. The parcel-based search region is kept for scoring.

D16 (2026-11-12). When no shipped reference applies and the local set has fewer than 30 subjects, no z-maps are computed; subjects are ranked on the raw ceiling fraction and the raw occipital-to-control regional thickness ratio, and the output says so. Reason: a per-vertex median and MAD from a handful of subjects is not meaningful. Whether to ship a provisional reference built from unrated census subjects stays open under O7.

D17 (2026-11-12). The code is licensed BSD-3-Clause. The licence for released ratings stays open under O12.

D18 (2026-11-12). Working conventions. Geometry is in mm throughout, so volumes that are not 1 mm conformed are handled. The T1 is upsampled by nearest neighbour, as in Freeview. "Deterministic" (hard rule in `CLAUDE.md`) means for a given input, config and locked environment (`uv.lock`); golden tests use a tolerance. The catalog uses stdlib `sqlite3` with `PRAGMA user_version` migrations, and `sqlmodel` stays in the `app` extra. The version stays static until the first tagged release. Regional metrics, ceiling fraction and scoring are built in milestone 3 because `review` orders its queue by the composite score; the intensity metric follows milestone 4. CI runs lint and tests on Linux and macOS for Python 3.11 and 3.13.

D19 (2026-11-12). Choices made while building milestone 1, with their reasons.

- Orientation check. nibabel's vox2ras-tkr ignores the direction cosines, so the documented test (tkr matrix not a signed permutation) could never fail on a file. The renderer refuses a volume whose scanner affine and tkr affine differ in their linear part, that is, any volume not in conformed LIA orientation.
- Contours are joined into polylines before drawing (see `rendering.md`); `LineCollection` of separate segments gave unevenly heavy lines.
- The video setting is part of a render's identity: asking for a render without video in a directory that has one renders again and removes the video.
- `vertex_map.npz` is stored uncompressed with fixed entry dates, for byte identity across zlib builds (1.8 MB instead of about 1.3 MB on one subject; with the selection rule of the coordinates review below, 2.5 MB instead of 1.7 MB from `numpy.savez_compressed`).
- The video is converted with the BT.709 matrix, limited range, and tagged, so players need not guess; swscale's bit-exact path is forced because its SIMD path gave different bytes.
- `three_plane(subject, "x.mp4")` renders into a hidden temporary directory next to the target and moves the video into place.
- `render.json` records an input only under a name that FreeSurfer gives a file of that role. A link to a pial variant in the same directory (`lh.pial -> lh.pial.T1`, FreeSurfer 7) is recorded under the variant's name. A link to anything else, in the same or in another directory, is recorded under the link's own name, and a file handed to `render_subject` under a name that is not the role's (a download cache may do that) under the role's first name. So no foreign name reaches `render.json`. Before the review a link such as `T1.mgz -> ds000999_sub-01_T1.mgz` in the same directory put the target's name into `render.json`.
- A directory is treated as an fsaverage template if its own name or the name of its link target starts with `fsaverage`.
- Tests run the package under jaxtyping and beartype with the PEP 484 numeric tower (an `int` passes where `float` is annotated), in agreement with the static checker.
- Byte identity of renders was checked on one machine across Python 3.11 (numpy 2.4) and 3.13 (numpy 2.5): identical media bytes. Not checked across machines or operating systems.

Added in the review of milestone 1 (robustness, 2026-11-12):

- Renders into one media directory are serialized by `flock` on the directory itself. Chosen over a lock file: it leaves nothing in the directory that the completeness check, the cleanup or the list of a render's files would have to know about, and the kernel releases it when the process dies, so a killed render cannot leave the directory locked. A lock file made with `O_EXCL` would be portable, but it stays behind after a kill and then needs a rule for when it is stale. Where `flock` is missing (Windows) or refused, the render runs without a lock, as it did before. Measurements and what was not checked (macOS, NFS) are in `rendering.md`.
- Cleanup removes files by the names a render writes, not by glob over the directory: a user's `.notes.tmp` or `tiles/mine.webp` in the output directory used to be deleted. Temporary files are removed only under the lock. This review still removed any `anim.mp4` in a render without video and did not refuse the subject directory as output; both were changed when the fixes were verified (below).
- `RenderConfig` checks colours (`#RRGGBB`) and bounds `line_width_px` by `panel_px / 8`, and `render_subject` validates the config again, since `model_copy(update=...)` skips validation. Before, a bad colour or a very wide line failed while drawing, after `render.json` of a complete render had been removed. The bound of an eighth is a choice: 40 px at the default panel, 2 px at the smallest (16 px), where the default line of 1.25 px still passes.
- Absent and unreadable are told apart by the package's own `stat()` probe (`sources/patterns.py`), not by `Path.is_file()`: for a file in a directory without search permission, `is_file()` raises `PermissionError` on Python 3.11.18 and 3.13.18 and returns False on 3.14.6 (run 2026-11-12 on Linux without `CAP_DAC_OVERRIDE`). `resolve_subject_files` and `is_freesurfer_subject_dir` raise `OSError` for a path they cannot examine, and the renderer turns that into `render failure`. They do not answer "absent" or "not a subject", because a scan would then drop an unreadable subject without a trace. The scan of milestone 2 has to catch the error for each directory and record it as unreadable. A name too long for the file system counts as absent.
- With a video target, `three_plane` does every check before it creates the parent directory or the temporary directory, and removes the directories it created if the render fails afterwards. The command line handles SIGTERM like Ctrl-C; a render killed with SIGKILL still leaves its hidden temporary directory next to the target.

Added in the review of milestone 1 (determinism, 2026-11-12):

- The contour renderer resets `path.sketch` on its artists, so a `matplotlibrc` or an earlier `plt.xkcd()` cannot change a tile. Chosen over drawing inside `matplotlib.rc_context` with default settings: that would change global state for the length of a draw, which is not safe with two threads rendering in one process, and the review's sweep of all rcParams found no other setting that reaches the tile. Measurements are in `rendering.md`.
- Config colours have one validation, a field validator that requires `#RRGGBB` and stores the upper-case form; it replaces the pattern check added in the robustness review. Lower-case colours used to give another config hash for the same bytes, so a change of letter case in a run config would have rendered every subject again. The hash of the default config did not change (`dff40fe66869`): the defaults were already upper case, and a validation rule is not part of the hashed payload. A test pins that value, since it may change only when the serialized value of a default or the schema version changes.
- `vox2pix` is the inverse of `pix2vox` in closed form, with negative zeros removed from both matrices. `np.linalg.inv` gave the last bit of the shifts and a `-0.0` of the LAPACK that numpy is linked with, which `uv.lock` does not pin (the review notes OpenBLAS in the Linux wheel and Accelerate in the macOS arm64 wheel, and reproduced other digits with reference LAPACK 3.12 on Linux). Checked on this machine on 10 renders before and after the change (synthetic subjects at 1, 0.8 and 0.7 mm and `ds000102` sub-01, panels of 64 to 384 px, both display orientations): every tile, poster, video and vertex map is byte-identical, and `render.json` differs only in `vox2pix`: `-0.0` became `0.0` in every render (one entry in the axial panel, four entries with the flipped orientations), and the last digit of a shift changed in 3 of the 10 (0.8 mm at the default panel, sub-01 at 384 px and at 334 px; for example `-160.0` became `-160.00000000000003`, the value the review got from reference LAPACK). Not removed: contour coordinates still pass through `np.linalg.inv` of the vox2ras-tkr affine (`geometry.tkr_to_pixel`), so tile bytes could in principle depend on LAPACK in the last bit of a coordinate. The review rendered three subjects with reference LAPACK and found all media files identical. Not checked on macOS.
- When the video is asked for, the ffmpeg binary is looked up before the volume and the surfaces are loaded and before anything is written, and a missing one is a `RenderError` with reason `render failure`. Before, `IMAGEIO_FFMPEG_EXE` pointing at a missing file surfaced as `FileNotFoundError` after `render.json` of a complete render had been removed and all tiles written again. The reason is one of the protocol's unratable reasons, although the cause lies in the environment and not in the subject: the census render phase (milestone 5) must not record it as a property of the subject. `write_mp4` now raises `RuntimeError`, as its documentation says, also for a binary that cannot be started and for one that exits with status 0 without a video. Both used to raise `OSError`, and a binary that exited with status 0 and left an empty file would have replaced an existing `anim.mp4` with it.
- The command-line tests do not depend on the terminal. Typer hands usage errors and help pages to Rich and forces Rich's terminal output when `GITHUB_ACTIONS`, `FORCE_COLOR` or `PY_COLORS` is set; Rich then puts escape codes inside option names (`--no-video` in three pieces), and it wraps to the terminal width. At the reviewed commit that failed `test_no_video_contradicts_a_video_path`, in every cell of the CI matrix according to the review; the test of an empty `--out` added in the robustness review failed the same way (run here with `GITHUB_ACTIONS=true` and `FORCE_COLOR=1` before the change: 3 failed, 9 passed in `tests/test_cli_render.py`). The tests now read the output with escape codes and frame removed and white space collapsed, and each test that reads such output runs three times (plain, forced colour at 80 columns, forced colour at 30 columns) with the variables Rich reads set by the fixture, so the CI condition is tested in every environment. Not chosen: disabling Rich for the tests (`_TYPER_FORCE_DISABLE_TERMINAL`), which would leave the output users get on CI untested.

Added in the review of milestone 1 (coordinates, 2026-11-12):

- `vertex_map.npz` is selected by what the tiles show, not by the view box, and D15 is refined accordingly. The rule, the measurements on `ds000102` sub-01 and the cost (1.8 to 2.5 MB for the file, 10.2 to 10.9 MB of media per subject) are in `rendering.md`.
- The region is the union of the three panels' boxes, not the cube of the field of view. The cube is simpler and nearly the same size (with a 5 mm margin 75,435 vertices on sub-01 against 74,654), but its eight corner blocks are shown by no tile: they lie outside the slice range of all three panels. 602 pial vertices of sub-01 lie there, and `visible` must be false for them, or D15 would count them as inspected.
- The region is grown box by box along the axes, not by Euclidean distance. That keeps a few vertices near the edges and corners of the grown boxes that no mark can reach, and it lets a reader recompute the selection from `render.json` with comparisons alone.
- Half a slice step means half of `slice_step_mm`, also where the slice planes are farther apart or closer than the step because the voxels are not the size of the step. It matters only at the two ends of each panel's sweep.
- `vertex_map_margin_mm` keeps its name and changes its meaning: the margin beyond the clickable region (default 5 mm, the snap radius) instead of the margin beyond the box (default 10 mm). The file gained arrays and `render.json` gained the counts of visible vertices, so the render schema version went from 1 to 2. The version is part of every config hash, so a render made under the old meaning cannot pass for an up-to-date render of a config that sets the margin to 10, which would otherwise hash like the old default. (The new counts are required fields, so an old `render.json` also fails to load; the version says why.) The hash of the default config changed from `dff40fe66869` to `17f83a0c9b6c`, and the test that pins it was updated. Every existing render is rendered again on the next request; a test does that with a render.json and vertex map in the form of version 1.
- `vertexmap.build_vertex_map` takes the region as an array of boxes instead of the view box, and `read_vertex_map` returns the `visible` arrays with the rest.
- `visible` is defined on the pial position alone. A vertex whose white position is shown and whose pial position is not is in the file and is not visible. D15 concerns pial capture; if white-surface marks (class C) get their own spatial analysis, it needs its own flag.
- Not done here: the app (milestone 4) has to refuse a snap radius larger than the `vertex_map_margin_mm` of the render it serves. Measured on one real subject (FreeSurfer 6.0.1, 1 mm) and on synthetic subjects at 1 and 0.8 mm; no FreeSurfer 7 subject.

Added in the review of milestone 1 (tests and docs, 2026-11-12):

- The review ran the tests against copies of the package with one thing broken in each, and found broken copies that passed all of them. Eleven tests (eighteen cases) were added and four existing ones tightened, so that each of these now fails at least one; the package code did not change for them. The broken copies: only the first, or only the first two, closed loops of a surface drawn on a tile; the crosshair of frame 0 on every frame of video and poster, or on the poster alone, the crosshair at another coronal slice, and no edge letters; an orientation tolerance of 5e-2, 1e-3 or 1e-5 in place of 1e-4; in `render.json`, a voxel size of 1 mm for every volume, two dimensions of the shape exchanged, and the left pole's vertex number for the right pole; an exact half in the synchronized frame table rounded up or down; the sagittal panel half a pixel off when `sagittal_anterior_left` is set; the video with the BT.601 matrix, without `-threads 1`, with preset `ultrafast`, or without `-flags:v +bitexact`; an input hashed over its first MiB only; an input name that may be a path; a NaN voxel that reaches the cast to `uint8`.
- The folded test mesh is a closed surface, a sphere whose radius is modulated into six gyri, which a sagittal plane crosses in three loops. `architecture.md` had planned a folded slab; the surface does the same job with a mesh the tests already build. Every other synthetic surface is an ellipsoid and gives one loop per tile.
- The network test compares the line coverage of every contour tile with the length of the contours on the tile times the line width, computed from the slicer. Measured on `ds000102` sub-01: 0.952 to 1.005 per tile, 0.976 over all tiles; on 152 of the 155 tiles with contours a surface gives more than one line, 15 at most. With only the first closed loop of each surface drawn the same ratios are 0.37 for the worst tile and 0.88 over all tiles, and the test as it was before (more than 80 % of tiles have some contour) passed. The test also checks size and SHA-256 of the six inputs, which are 1.7 to 5.6 MB; no synthetic input was larger than 0.4 MB.
- The encoder settings are checked on the ffmpeg command line. Preset and bit-exact flags do not show in a file that is made and read back on one machine, so no test of the output can pin them. The thread count is tested by its effect as well: ffmpeg is shown one processor and then all of them (`os.sched_setaffinity`), and the two files must be identical. That test needs Linux and more than one processor and is skipped elsewhere, so in CI it runs in the Linux cells only.
- The orientation tolerance is tested on both sides: a scanner affine turned by 5e-5 rad is rendered, 2e-4 rad is refused. The render of the turned volume has the tiles, poster, video and vertex map of the unturned one, byte for byte; only `render.json` differs. Image and contours are both placed through vox2ras-tkr, which nibabel builds without the direction cosines, so the tolerance does not decide whether contours sit on the image. It decides how far the edge letters, the pole anchor and the box may be turned against the anatomy.
- The geometry tests had no case with `sagittal_anterior_left` set since D13 changed its default: the case named `posterior_left` was the default config. The cases now cover both switches both ways, and a test says so.
- `README.md` describes what is built (`render-subject` and `render.three_plane`) and lists `review`, `export` and the census as planned. It had shown only commands that do not exist.

Added in the final check of the milestone 1 review (2026-11-12):

- A damaged input is reported like one that may not be opened: `unreadable input (<file name>: <error>)`, with the file's path removed from the error text. Before, the text of nibabel's exception was passed on as it was, and for an empty `T1.mgz` or `brainmask.mgz` it holds the whole path (`Empty file: '/.../mri/T1.mgz'`), against the rule of the robustness review that the message names the file and not the path. Tried on a synthetic subject with a truncated, an empty and a garbage `T1.mgz`, a garbage brainmask, and a truncated, an empty and a garbage surface: only the two empty volumes carried a path.
- `RenderConfig` refuses a view box that no panel can show: a low end above the high end along an axis, or no extent along any axis together with a `margin_mm` of 0. Before, such a config was accepted and then refused once per subject as `render failure` ("the view box lies outside the volume", "no extent"), which a census would have recorded as a failure of every subject. A box that is flat along one or two axes is still allowed. The check in `geometry.field_of_view` stays for a config that was not validated.
- The four reviews together were checked on `ds000102` sub-01: all 352 tiles, `poster.webp` and `anim.mp4` are byte-identical to the render made before the review (schema 1); `vertex_map.npz` (2,465,892 bytes, 74,654 vertices) and `render.json` differ as described above; 10.9 MB of media in 356 files, rendered in 8.7 to 9.3 s on one core (three runs).

Added in the verification of the review fixes (2026-11-12). Independent checks of the fixes above found seven problems; what was done about each:

- `anim.mp4` belongs to a render only if the `render.json` in the directory lists a video. A render without video used to remove any `anim.mp4`, also in a directory that was never rendered into (`render-subject SUBJECT --out . --no-video` in a directory with a film of that name). The earlier `render.json` is read as plain JSON, so that a render of schema 1, which does not load under the present model, still gives up its video. The video is removed before `render.json`, not at the end of the render with the stale tiles: a render without video that is interrupted would otherwise leave the earlier video with no `render.json` to say whose it is. One such case remains: a render with video that is interrupted leaves an `anim.mp4`, the earlier one or its own, and a later render without video then leaves it in place, unlisted. Chosen over a marker file, which every other part of the cleanup would have to know.
- An output in a source directory is refused (`OutputInSourceError`, a `ValueError`; exit status 2 on the command line). The robustness review had left this to milestone 2. It is done now because the case that loses a file needs no run directory: `render-subject sub-01 --out sub-01 --no-video` removed an `anim.mp4` in the subject directory, and any render there adds files to a source, against the hard rule. The rule is applied in `render_subject`, which sees only files, so it works from where the files are: the subject directory for a file at its FreeSurfer place, else the directory that holds the file, and for a link the directory of its target. An output directory that holds the subject directory is accepted, since nothing in the subject directory changes; a `SUBJECTS_DIR` given as `--out` gets `tiles/` and `render.json` next to its subjects.
- The pattern for a render's tiles had `\d`, which in Python matches every Unicode decimal digit, and allowed any number of leading zeros. `tiles/axial_٠٠٠.webp` and `tiles/axial_0001.webp` were removed as stale tiles although the renderer cannot write either name. The pattern now has ASCII digits: three, or four and more without a leading zero.
- Cleanup removes files and links only. A directory under the name of a stale tile (`tiles/coronal_998.webp/`) made `unlink` raise `IsADirectoryError` after `render.json` was removed and every tile rewritten, in this render and in every later one. Not changed: a directory under a name that the render does write (`tiles/coronal_000.webp/`) still fails as an `OSError` when the tile is moved into place, like any output location that cannot be written.
- `visible` keeps its rule (the pial position lies in the clickable region), and the documents no longer say that it holds every vertex at which a drawn contour can appear. Measured on `ds000102` sub-01: 1,181 of 373,536 pixels of the drawn pial contour (0.32 %) resolve to a vertex that is not visible, at most 1.07 mm outside the region. The other rule was measured as well: with the corners of every pial face that a slice plane cuts inside the panel's field of view added, 57,484 vertices are visible instead of 55,534 and none of the 373,536 pixels resolves outside them. It was not adopted. A mark need not lie on the contour, since it snaps over up to 5 mm, so no rule for `visible` makes every mark's vertex visible, and the analysis has to say what it does with such a mark in either case (`analysis_plan.md`: the mark is kept, its own vertex is not counted). The region rule can be recomputed from `render.json` by comparisons, which a rule on cut faces cannot once the surfaces are deleted; a pial face of sub-01 is up to 7.5 mm long (median edge 0.95 mm), so its corners can lie well outside what is shown; and the flags of every existing render would change, which is another schema version. If the pilot shows marks at tile edges to matter, this is the place to change.
- Two tests did not fail when the thing they are named for was removed. The test for negative zeros had no case with a shift of zero; a case with a tile edge on voxel coordinate 0 was added (the default box around x = 88 in a conformed volume). The test of a schema 1 render used a `render.json` that does not load under the present model, so it was rendered again whatever the version logic did; it now also runs on files that load, with the version number of schema 1, with its config hash, and with both. Each new and each changed test of this round was run against a copy of the package with its fix undone, and fails there.

D20 (2026-11-12). The privacy boundary of D10 is built into what code can reach, not left to convention. Implementation, milestone 2:

D21 (2026-11-12). One skip rule for every phase, decided from the listing. A unit is skipped when its row says `done`, its config hash and input signature are those of the present configuration and listing, and its output exists. A failed unit with the same hashes is not tried again unless `--retry-failed`; `--force` ignores the row. The input signature is computed from role, location, size and ETag or modification time, and for a role with several candidate names from the name that was read, so the decision opens no source file. Outputs are written before the row, so a crash never leaves a row without its output. Rows live in the `work` ledger (keyed by phase and unit) and, for renders, in `renders`. Reasons: a phase that is interrupted or run twice redoes nothing that is done, and a census can be resumed per dataset. Consequences worked out in milestone 2:

- The inventory has no ledger rows; its skip test is `subjects.inventory_hash`, the signature over all listed files with the inventory stage hash. Two cases add lines to that hash: a directory that could not be listed adds its problem text, and a subject whose build stamp, log or stats file could not be read gets a marker that no listing gives, so the next inventory reads it again. An unread Tier A file is not a failed unit. A dataset whose subjects cannot be listed is a failed unit `dataset:<pseudonym>`; its subjects are not marked absent, and the detail is in the report notes and the log only.
- Frames from the catalog and from `read_table` have fixed column types whatever the rows hold: text is `object` with None, a flag that may be unknown is pandas `boolean`, an integer that may be missing a pandas nullable integer. Parquet tables are sorted by their key and then by the remaining columns, and a NaN is written as a missing value, so the bytes of a table depend on its set of rows and the locked pyarrow only. Checked on Python 3.11 and 3.13 on one machine.
- `Catalog.mark_absent` sets `present` from the listing in both directions. `Catalog.relocate` refuses data of another pipeline, because the pipeline is hashed into `subject_uid`. When the data moved into a layout with another `fs_root`, it moves `subjects.fs_dir` of every subject of the dataset in the same transaction: the inventory skips a subject whose files are unchanged, and `inventory_hash` does not cover `fs_dir`, so no later phase would correct it. For the same reason the inventory's skip test compares the stored `fs_dir` beside the hash: subjects that moved, at the same root, into another layout of the same pipeline (`freesurfer/sub-01` to `sub-01`) are stored again and keep their `subject_uid`, renders and ratings. A dataset never changes its pseudonym: a different one for a stored dataset is treated as a wrong key.
- Data that is found in the layout of another pipeline at the same root (a subjects directory moved into `sourcedata/freesurfer/`, an fMRIPrep output written beside a `freesurfer/` folder, which the layout rules then prefer) is a second dataset row with the same pseudonym, and its subjects get new `subject_uid`s, because the pipeline is hashed into them. The subjects of the earlier row are no longer listed: the inventory marks them absent, sets the row's listed count to 0 and says so in a note, once the new listing has succeeded. Before this they stayed present and every subject counted twice. Their renders and ratings stay under the old identifiers and are not carried over. When the earlier pipeline was `freesurfer`, the earlier subjects come back with their renders by giving the directory that holds the subject directories as `--source` with `--relocate`, since such a directory is always read as a plain subjects directory (tried once, on a subjects directory moved into `sourcedata/freesurfer/`); there is no such way back to an earlier `fmriprep` row once the fMRIPrep layout is gone. `status` names a pseudonym once. `--relocate` moves the rows of the pipeline found at the new place and takes the root of an earlier row along.

Local source.

- A path that is not a directory is `SourceError("not found")`, one that cannot be listed is `unreadable`; `layout` means a readable directory without subjects. The contract named only `layout`.
- A subject glob that matches nothing is reported when the subjects are listed, not when the source is built, so a run whose stored glob no longer matches can still be opened. The command line checks a glob before it creates a run or stores the glob.
- One subject directory as path: the source is its parent restricted to that name. Globs given as well must match the name.
- A subject directory whose name is not valid UTF-8 (Latin-1 bytes, which Linux file systems take) is skipped with a warning. A label is hashed into the `subject_uid` and stored as text, and such a name can be neither; before, it stopped every inventory of its dataset with a `UnicodeEncodeError`. Renaming the directory is the way to include it. Tried on one Linux file system only.
- `field_strength` is positive and finite: `LocalSource` raises `ValueError` and `SourceConfig` refuses the value, also for infinity; the command line turns either into `invalid: ...` with exit code 2, on a new run as on a stored one.
- `template_location` is recorded when an `fsaverage` directory exists next to the subjects, also when it lacks template files, so the template lookup can name what is missing; `template_dir()` returns it only when all eight files are present. `template_files_missing()` and `LocalSource.subjects_dir` were added.
- A subject directory reached through a link keeps the link's name as its label. A folder named `fmriprep` next to `freesurfer/` is recognised by the name as given and by the name of what it links to.
- Paths are built from the source's own root, not from the root in a record, so a record from a catalog that still names an earlier place is served from where the data is. `FileRef.variant` is set only for roles with several candidate names; `render.json` names its inputs through the same function (`patterns.variant_name`), where the renderer had its own copy of the rule of D19 before the review. A local source ignores the `only` argument of `datasets()`.
- `read_bytes` raises `not found` for a role the subject lacks, `changed` for a file that is gone or differs from the listing, `unreadable` for one that cannot be opened; the detail holds the location relative to the subject, never an absolute path. Modification times are compared in nanoseconds. Run on one Linux file system only.

Provenance. The rules were fixed on the logs of two real subjects and on `scripts/recon-all` at v6.0.0 and v7.4.1 (`data_sources.md`); excerpts of the six logs, with directory names, accession and host replaced, are test data.

- Flag names: besides tokens of the form `-name`, `-3T` and `-3t` are kept (real flags, the only ones that start with a digit), and a flag-shaped token that contains an accession is dropped.
- T2pial, FLAIRpial and hires use the spellings that recon-all's argument parser takes (`-T2pial`, `-t2pial`, `-T2pial-only`, `-autorecon3-T2pial`; off: `-noT2pial`, `-no-T2pial`, `-no_T2pial` and the lower-case forms; likewise FLAIR; `-hires`, `-highres`). recon-all has no flag that clears `-hires`.
- `finished` is the verdict of the last invocation, not of the last verdict line in the file: a log whose last invocation was killed is not finished.
- A flag is stored as false only when the logs can show it (every log the subject has was read in full, and an invocation was found); otherwise an unseen flag is unknown. The contract had unknown only for "no log read".
- A bare carriage return ends a line (the logs hold progress output). Numbers in the patterns are bounded in length, because nothing in this module may raise on any text. With a partial text, a last line without a line end is ignored.
- The version comes from the build stamp file, else from the stamp line of `recon-all.log`, also when the file's stamp holds no version. Holes come from `recon-all.log`, else from a per-hemisphere log.
- The contract's statement that the 7.3.2 log does not separate invocations was wrong: it writes `#New# invocation of recon-all`.
- Not checked: a log of a plain `recon-all -all` run; which of the two truncation counts is white-to-pial; where `-T2pial` appears when a T2 was used; `-autorecon2-pial`, which is in neither version read and is kept as an edit flag from memory.

Catalog.

- Beside the methods of the contract, `Catalog` has `query`, `pseudonym` and `scrub_terms`, and the module has `media_dir`, `table_path`, `part_path` and a `schema` argument on the part functions (the intensity table of a run with other depths has its own schema).
- `upsert_dataset` keeps a stored derivative ref, snapshot tag and subject count when given None. `upsert_subject` takes the digests of the files that the inventory read in full (a keyword argument that the contract did not have) and writes them with the subject; a file that is not among them keeps its digest while its location, variant, size and ETag or modification time are unchanged. It stores `recon_flags` as NULL when no log was read, and raises `ConfigError("invalid")` on a collision of two subjects on one `subject_uid` and `ConfigError("key")` when a subject is stored under another one.
- `PublicCatalog.meta` raises for a key that is not public, so a request for the run secret is loud. `Catalog.set_meta` refuses to change what identifies the run.
- `Catalog.failures` also lists unreadable subjects (phase `inventory`) and failed renders (phase `render`).
- A migration is split into statements by line and run in one transaction; `executescript` would commit on its own.
- One connection per catalog, usable from several threads behind a lock (the rater app answers from a thread pool). Methods cannot be called inside an open `write()` block.
- A catalog that another connection keeps locked for longer than the busy timeout is reported as in use (`ConfigError("invalid", "catalog.sqlite is in use by another process; ...")`), on opening and at the start of a write. Before, opening reported it as "not a SQLite database", which invites deleting the catalog, the only copy of the run secret. The wait on opening is the 60 s of a census run also for a local run, because the mode is read from the file.
- When a write fails, the transaction is rolled back only if SQLite has not ended it already. On a full disk it has, and the rollback then raised "cannot rollback - no transaction is active" in place of "database or disk is full". Reproduced with `PRAGMA max_page_count` and on a 64 kB tmpfs.
- Not checked: write-ahead mode on a real network file system; the list of file system types treated as network mounts is from memory; the two-process write test ran on one local disk.

Batches.

- An exception comes back from a worker process as an instance of its own class. The package's error classes take `(reason, detail)`, and pickle could not read them back: a process pool that received one was broken for the whole batch (seen with `SourceError` on Python 3.13). `run_batches` carries exceptions as class, arguments and attributes, and `SourceError`, `ConfigError` and `RenderError` now also pickle on their own. An exception that cannot be sent arrives as `batches.WorkerError`.
- A subject whose worker process dies gets `BrokenProcessPool`; the subjects lost with it are run again one at a time, so a work function must be safe to repeat.
- Workers ignore SIGINT: on Ctrl-C the parent waits for running work, releases the leases and stops, and the interrupted batch is not committed.
- Log records of the package logger made in a worker are logged in the parent when the subject finishes, so a filter on the package logger sees them. The progress bar is drawn only when stderr is a terminal.
- A work function that cannot be pickled is a `TypeError` before anything is leased.

Inventory. See D21 for the hash. Also: a listed subject with a problem is a failed unit whether it was stored now or skipped, so a second run reports the same count; a pseudonym in `--dataset` that no source yields is a failed unit; two sources that yield the same dataset are refused; `field_strength` of a source is written to the listed subjects, also to skipped ones, so a changed `--field-strength` needs no `--force`. `dataset_files` is not written yet. Behaviour with a remote source is tested only with a local source posing as one.

Added in the review of milestone 2 (2026-11-12), two findings on what a stored subject traces back to:

- The name of the file that was read is part of the input signature (D21). A FreeSurfer 7 subject has `surf/lh.pial` as a link to `lh.pial.T1`, or to `lh.pial.T2` after a T2 refinement. The role's location is the link, the variants of one subject have the same size, and a copy that keeps the modification time (`cp -p`) has the same time, so a link pointed at another variant gave the same signature: the inventory skipped the subject and kept `pial_variant = pial.T1` and `t2pial = 0`, and the render of the subject was skipped as well, so its tiles stayed those of the other surface. Reproduced in tests on synthetic trees, for the inventory and for the render; it needs the equal modification time, so it is rare. The signature of every subject changes with this, since the pial roles always have a variant: a catalog made before it inventories every subject once more, and its renders are found complete on disk and recorded again without rendering. For the same reason `upsert_subject` drops a kept digest when the variant changed.
- The build stamp and the three logs get their SHA-256 in `files.sha256` when the inventory read them whole, as the files of a render do. Before, `recon_flags`, `n_invocations`, the holes and the truncation counts traced to a size and a modification time only, which for a local file do not say which bytes were parsed. A log of which only the head was read (`inventory.logs = "head"`) gets no digest, and keeps one from an earlier full reading while its listing is unchanged. The stats files are not read by the inventory and get none until the regional metrics of milestone 3 read them. The digests are written in the transaction that stores the subject; written after it, an interruption in between would leave a subject that every later inventory skips without them. Checked on the two real subjects of the network tests (`ds000102` sub-01, `ds003020` sub-UTS01), linked into a scratch subjects directory: the eight files have in the catalog the SHA-256 of their bytes, no other file has a digest, and a second inventory skips both subjects.

Rendering a run (`render/batch.py`).

- `run_render` has no `template` argument yet; milestone 3 adds it with `templates.py`. `RenderError` gained `detail`, since a run stores reason and detail in separate columns.
- Refused before anything is recorded against a subject (`ConfigError`): a video without a working encoder, a video with an odd `panel_px`, a `subject_uid` the run does not have, and a run directory inside a subject directory.
- What lies in the machine and not in a subject stops the phase with exit code 2 and is not recorded, so that the plain command does the work once the cause is gone. A failed row with the same hashes is not tried again without `--retry-failed`, and such a cause fails every subject alike. Two cases beyond the missing ffmpeg binary of the contract. The encoder: before the first batch of a render with video, ffmpeg encodes two 16 px frames into a temporary directory (about 20 ms here), which also catches a binary that starts and cannot encode. The media: an `OSError` from a render with `ENOSPC`, `EDQUOT` or `EROFS`, or with `EACCES` or `EPERM` on a path under `media/`, and a failure of ffmpeg whose text is that of the first three (ffmpeg writes the video itself and reports in words), end the call at that batch; nothing of the batch is recorded, and the renders of it that did succeed are found complete on disk by the next call. The inputs cannot cause these, because `render_subject` turns every error of reading them into a `RenderError`. Tried on tmpfs mounts as root: a full and a read-only run directory, `media/` on a full file system with and without video, an ffmpeg that exits with 1. Not covered: a disk that fills while ffmpeg writes and that ffmpeg reports in other words (the text was seen with the ffmpeg of imageio-ffmpeg on Linux only), and any other error of writing the output (`EIO`, a file where a directory should be), which are still recorded as `render failure` for the subject and need `--retry-failed`.
- A render that is complete on disk and has no row (a crash between the files and the row) is found complete by `render_subject`, which does nothing, and is then recorded.
- `render_status` says what a call would find now (done, failed, pending) without rendering; `surfcensus status` uses it. It was split off from `run_render` so that the two cannot disagree. `render_failures` names the subjects in the failed count with their reasons, and for an incomplete subject that has no `missing surface` row yet the detail that `run_render` will record.
- The check sample is drawn from the sorted `subject_uid`s of the done renders; the text file names the seed in its first line. It is written to `logs/` in a local run as well.

`Run` and the command line.

- `relocate` is a flag or the pseudonym of the dataset that moved (the contract had a flag and left the pseudonym to the command line). It replaces the root, and with it where the subjects lie under the root when the layout changed; the stored subject globs and field strength stay. A source given on opening whose root matches a stored one replaces that source's globs and field strength: from Python the source given is the truth. On the command line `--source` on an existing local run only says which source is meant, and `--subjects` and `--field-strength` change the stored values.
- `identity` of a local source is fixed when the run is created, not at the first inventory; the two differ only if the data moves in between.
- Sources are built when a phase first needs them, so `status` works on a run whose data is not mounted. A source that cannot be built stops `inventory` and `render` with exit code 2 before anything is recorded: a missing disk is not a property of its subjects. A `render` that finds nothing pending builds no source and reports its counts, so a finished run can be checked with the data unmounted; `Run.render` asks `render_pending` first, which plans the call a second time from the catalog.
- A run is created only in a new or empty directory, and by one process: `config.yaml` is written exclusively (a hard link from a temporary file, or an exclusive open on a file system without hard links), before the catalog. Of two processes that create a run in one directory at the same time, the one that comes second is refused with `invalid: ... already holds a run` and changes nothing. Before, it replaced the salt in `config.yaml` and was then refused by the catalog, which left a run that no command could open. In 40 races of two `inventory --source` commands after the change, one command was refused 7 times and no run was left unusable. A process that opened the run in the short time before its catalog was filled could still be refused with a message about the catalog; since the final check of the milestone (below) it gets the run. A place where no run can be made (no permission, a read-only or full disk) is `ConfigError("invalid", "no run can be made in ...")`; a `config.yaml` left without a catalog by a full disk is a run whose catalog the next call makes.
- A run directory inside the subjects directory is refused, also for one subject directory as path, whose subjects directory is its parent. A run directory beside the subjects directory inside an fMRIPrep or derivatives folder is allowed.
- A `config` given on opening replaces the stored one when it is of the same run (mode and identity); this is how stored options are changed. `config.with_changes` validates a changed configuration as a whole. `from_config` takes `root` and `identity` from its caller when given, because a census run stores neither for a local source.
- A census run can be created from Python with the dataset key, with local sources or none, and opened without the key; its `inventory` needs the key. No command creates one before milestone 5.
- In a local run `Run.render(subjects=...)` accepts labels as well as `subject_uid`s.
- `--relocate [PSEUDONYM]` takes an optional value, which typer cannot declare; the command rewrites a bare `--relocate` before parsing.
- `status` prints done and failed for the inventory and no pending count, which the catalog cannot know without listing the source; for the render, pending is what `render` would do now. It always ends with 0. The render lines of `--failures` are the subjects in the failed count (review of milestone 2). Before, the list was every failed row of `renders`: after an inventory and before any render, `status` said `1 failed` for a subject without `lh.pial` and listed nothing, and a failure recorded for a subject that had since left the source, or that was pending again with other files or another configuration, was listed without being counted. Details of recorded failures still come from the private catalog.
- A phase command stopped by Ctrl-C ends with 130, not with the 1 that means failed units.
- An `OSError` or `sqlite3.OperationalError` that reaches a phase command (a catalog on a read-only disk, a disk that fills during a commit) is printed as one line `invalid: the run directory cannot be used: ...` with exit code 2, not as a traceback with exit code 1, which means failed units. Sources and renders turn their own errors into `SourceError` and failed units, so what arrives there is the run's. An inventory that changes nothing still writes the dataset row, so on a read-only run directory it stops this way, while `status` and a `render` with nothing to do work.
- Summary lines name subjects by label in a local run and by `subject_uid` in a census run.

Added in the final check of milestone 2 (2026-11-13). Independent checks of the review fixes found four problems, each reproduced before it was changed:

- A run whose creation was killed could be unusable for good, and a run opened while it was being created could be refused. `Catalog.create` had claimed the name `catalog.sqlite` with an empty file and then written the schema and the identity of the run into it in two transactions. A process killed in between left a file that every later command migrated and then refused as "not made by surfcensus", which reads as a foreign file to delete; a second process that opened the run in that time got the same message. Of 80 creations killed after 0 to 30 ms, 13 left such a run. Now the catalog is built under a temporary name in the run directory, with the schema, the identity and the run secret in one transaction, and gets its name through a hard link, which fails when the name is taken. `catalog.sqlite` therefore holds a run or does not exist. A process that opens a run whose catalog is not there yet makes the catalog itself, with the salt of `config.yaml`; whichever of the two links first has made the catalog and the other takes it (`exist_ok`). After the change, of 200 creations killed the same way 170 opened and 30 had no `config.yaml` yet, and a `status` started inside the widened window ended with 0 on the run that the creating command then filled.
- Where the file system has no hard links, the name is taken with an empty file and the run is made in that file in one transaction, so a kill leaves an empty file, with or without a journal that SQLite rolls back. Such a file, and the tables without a row that a kill left under the earlier code, is an unfinished catalog (`store.UnfinishedCatalog`): opening it changes nothing, and opening the run makes the run in it under SQLite's write lock, after looking again. It is not removed and made again, as the check had proposed: between the look and the removal another process can have finished the same file, and its catalog would be deleted while it writes to it. A file with tables and no schema number, with a schema number and no `meta` table, or with a row in any table is not unfinished and is refused as before, unchanged. What a file is, is read in one read transaction: a test with four processes found the case where one read schema number 0, another then committed the run, and the first saw rows and called the file foreign.
- The switch of a census catalog to the write-ahead log comes after the transaction that makes the run, and SQLite refuses it while another connection reads or writes. That can only happen where the run is made in the catalog file itself. So every opening of a census catalog for writing asks for the switch again, which does nothing on a catalog that is in that mode. Measured with 6 processes that make one catalog at the same moment, 15 rounds for each of local and census and of four starts (no file, an empty file, tables without a row, no hard links): in all 120 rounds every process got the same run and wrote to the file that has the name, and every census catalog was in WAL mode afterwards. Before the later openings asked again, 10 of 36 census rounds on the three in-place starts ended in rollback mode.
- Not covered by this: the temporary files of a killed creation stay in the run directory under hidden names (`.catalog.sqlite.<token>.tmp`, `.config.yaml.<token>.tmp`; 48 files in the 200 killed creations) and are never read; they are not removed, since one may belong to a process that is still working. A census run with a local source keeps the root in the catalog only, so one killed before that row is written opens and then stops a phase with "the catalog has no dataset"; no command creates a census run yet. A catalog whose writer was killed in the middle of a commit is put right by the next opening for writing, as before; opened read-only before that (`PublicCatalog.open`, not the commands, which open for writing first) it is reported as "not a SQLite database: attempt to write a readonly database", which is wrong and was not changed here. Nothing of this was tried on a network file system or on a file system that really lacks hard links; that case is tested with `os.link` made to fail.
- A source whose root is not valid UTF-8 is refused when the source is made: `SourceError("invalid", ...)`, one line and exit code 2 on the command line, and no run directory. The earlier fix covered subject directory names only; a root of other bytes, a link that leads to one, and one subject directory inside one passed and then stopped the inventory with a `UnicodeEncodeError` from SQLite and exit code 1, the code of failed units. The root is what is tested, after links are resolved, because that is what the run stores and hashes; a link with a text name to one subject directory still works, with the link's directory as root. A subject glob that is not valid UTF-8 is refused the same way. `invalid` is a new reason of `SourceError`.
- A render that is recorded as done is skipped only when it is whole on disk (`render.api.render_on_disk`): the `render.json` of the present config and video setting, and a file under every name it lists. Before, `render.json` alone was looked for, so a media directory that had lost tiles or its poster (a partial copy, a restore from a backup) stayed done for good, in `render` and in `status`, and the rater app would have asked for files that are not there. `render_subject` has always made the full test; the plan of a run did not call it for a subject it skipped. The names are looked up in a listing of the media directory and one of `tiles/`, not file by file: 0.8 ms per render with 355 listed files on a local disk, against 3.0 ms for `Path.is_file` on each, so about 4 s for 5,000 renders by multiplication; not measured on a network file system. The listing says that a name is there, not that it is a file or what it holds; a damaged tile is not found by it.

Tests that CI does not run. CI runs the default tests (`uv run pytest`) and has neither a network job nor a kept pytest cache. D22 (2026-11-13). The fsaverage template is built at run time from an `fsaverage` directory, identified by content and pinned to the run. Nothing fsaverage-derived ships in the wheel: the condition of D14, that the FreeSurfer licence permits it, is not met. The directory is the one given with `--fsaverage`, else that of a FreeSurfer install, else the one next to the subjects (the order of D14 without its last step). The template's id is 12 hex characters over the mesh name, the region settings and the SHA-256 of the files that were read. On first use a run stores the template under `templates/<template_id>/`, records it in the catalog and keeps it: later calls read the cache whatever the environment looks like, a directory that builds another id stops the command unless `--retemplate`, and a run whose template can be neither read nor built again raises instead of falling back to the ranking of D16. Reason: a run's scores, clusters and click-mark vertices are only comparable on one mesh with one set of regions, and a volume that is not mounted must not change what a run computes. Without any template a run has no maps; it is ranked as D16 says and its renders carry no fsaverage vertices, with a message that says where a template was looked for and how to give one.

D24 (2026-11-13). Render schema 3: a render says which template its fsaverage counterparts refer to. `render.json` gained `template` (mesh, template id, the digests of the template spheres) and `fsaverage_poles` (decision D12), and lists the `sphere.reg` files among its inputs. The tiles do not depend on the template, and neither do `tile_hash` and `render_key` (decision D20), so a rating stays with its tiles when a template arrives or changes: such a render is brought up to date by writing `vertex_map.npz` and `render.json` alone. The schema number is part of every config hash, so every render of schema 2 is made again; none had a rating. A `sphere.reg` that is missing or cannot be used leaves its hemisphere without counterparts and is not a failure of the render.

D25 (2026-11-13). z-maps are computed on demand and not stored; cluster centroids reach the native surface through the vertex map. What is stored per subject is the float16 thickness map (164 kB); a z-map follows from it and from the reference (`Run.zmap`), and storing one per subject and reference would double the maps for each reference. A centroid's native vertex is found among the vertices of the render's `vertex_map.npz` whose nearest template vertex is the centroid (or a mesh neighbour of it), not by reading the subject's `sphere.reg` again. Reason: in a census the Tier B and C files are deleted after use, and the reference, and with it the clusters, is rebuilt after rating. The price: it is a nearest-vertex mapping, and a centroid outside what the tiles show has no native position.

D27 (2026-11-13). The composite standardises its components over the whole run by rank-based normal scores, and its cluster component is the excess mass. Reasons, each in `metrics.md`: standardising within a dataset would remove the differences between datasets that the census is about; a robust z has no usable scale for metrics that are 0 in most subjects; the plain sum of z jumps when a single vertex crosses the threshold, and noise makes clusters in every subject. Built in `metrics/scoring.py`: a cluster mass is standardised among the subjects scored against the same reference, every other component among the subjects of the run, each counted once; maxima and ties are decided on the float32 values that are stored. The other scale (`scoring.standardize = "robust_z"`), the other population (`"stratum"`) and the plain sum (`scoring.mass = "sum_z"`) are settings. The price is stated there too: the composite is a rank statistic, and on the 34 real subjects it was measured on, the ceiling component orders subjects by single vertices (O17).

D31 (2026-11-13). Choices made while building milestone 3 (stats and annotation readers, the sphere mapping, the template, the maps, the reference, clusters, ceiling counts, scores, the metric stages of a run, render schema 3), with their reasons. Where a choice departs from `docs/dev/contract.md`, it says so. The decisions of the contract's list for Tomas that this build rests on are in force as defaults and listed under O18.

Reading FreeSurfer files (`fsio/stats.py`, `fsio/annot.py`, `fsio/morph.py`).

- The stats parser keeps measure names with a number and a unit, the column names and the data rows, and nothing else of the header; an error names a line or a row by number and never quotes the file. Beyond the contract it refuses a data row without a `# ColHeaders` line above it and a second such line, so a file that is not a stats file cannot come back as rows of its text. A measure needs at least four comma fields, a unit of one word and a finite decimal number; of two lines with one name the first is kept. `surface_holes` gives no pair unless both counts are there and are counts. `aparc_frame` refuses a needed column that is named twice, a value that is no number, and a structure in two rows.
- `regional_rows` leaves out a parcel without a finite thickness, with a warning, because the table has no missing values, and returns its rows sorted by parcel, not in file order. The contract's "34 parcels per hemisphere" is what the two real subjects and the 34 of the measurement have; `mris_anatomical_stats` writes no row for a parcel without vertices, and nothing assumes 34. `region_mean_thickness` refuses rows of two subjects; `occ_ctrl_ratio` gives no ratio when the control mean is not positive.
- nibabel 5.5.0 reads some damaged files without an error (a thickness file cut short, an annotation cut inside its last colour, a value that the colour table lacks), so each reader checks the layout itself before nibabel reads the values; the contract asked only for nibabel. `read_annotation` maps annotation values to table entries itself (a value the table lacks is -1) and refuses a table whose entries are not numbered in order; names are decoded with replacement of bytes that are not UTF-8; the path that the colour table carries is never returned. `read_label` reads the text itself and returns unique indices. Errors are `OSError` for a file that cannot be opened and `ValueError`, starting with the file name and without the path, for a damaged one.

The sphere mapping (`fsio/registration.py`).

- A target within 1e-12 (on the unit sphere) of a source vertex takes that vertex alone, with weights (1, 0, 0) and without being a fallback. The contract's formula does not give exactly that in floating point, and the tests ask that a sphere mapped onto itself is the identity. So a row with three equal corners is not always a fallback: fallbacks are counted from the `fallback` array.
- A face that names a vertex outside the sphere is a `ValueError` (`degenerate sphere`), as is a sphere without vertices; the contract says no input raises but a degenerate sphere. In `resample`, a target that loses no weight to an invalid corner keeps its weights as they are, so all-valid flags give the bytes of no flags, and a value at a corner of weight 0 is never read. Ties go to the lowest index, also among the nearest candidates. An inward sphere is reversed with the corner order turned, so its map equals that of the outward sphere bit for bit.
- One map takes 0.36 to 0.42 s per hemisphere on the real subject; the contract said 0.2 s.

The template (`templates.py`); see D22.

- Vertex areas are shared in halves between the two parents of each dropped vertex, order by order, not given whole to the nearest target vertex as the contract has it. The nearest-vertex rule is decided by distance differences of half a percent and produced areas between 0.21 and 7.65 mm2 on the real left hemisphere; halves give 0.66 to 5.59 mm2 and the same total (`metrics.md`).
- `TemplateError` has two reasons beyond the contract's (`vertex count mismatch`, `unreadable`), and a place in `TemplateNotFound.looked_in` can read `unreadable: <error>`. Both hemispheres index one list of parcel names. A cached template on another mesh or with other regions than the configuration is `changed`.
- The id is over the bytes of the source files, as the contract says, and a surface file carries a creation line. The two fsaverage copies that were compared (next to `ds000102` and next to `ds003020`) therefore give two ids for the same arrays on fsaverage6 (`metrics.md`). Within a run this does not matter, because the run pins one template; references are matched by the digests of the spheres, which agree. Which copy a run with several sources pins follows the order of the pseudonyms, which differs from run to run: two runs over the 34 real subjects pinned one copy each, and every number of `metrics.md` came out the same.
- `store_template` and `load_template` were added: worker processes read the template from its directory in the run, once per process, instead of receiving its 6 MB with every subject.

Maps, reference and clusters (`metrics/fsaverage.py`, `metrics/clusters.py`, `reference/load.py`).

- `resample_hemi` returns the map as the float16 file will hold it, so the global median it reports is the one that can be recomputed from the file. `write_maps` writes a missing hemisphere as NaN and every NaN with the same bits. In `build_reference` a vertex outside the template cortex has a count of 0, smoothing fills no vertex, and medians are taken from sorted columns in float64, a block of vertices at a time. `zmap` gives NaN, not infinity, where the MAD is 0.
- Not built: reading the stack of a stratum in blocks of vertices above 8,000 subjects. The stack of one stratum and hemisphere is held in memory as float32, 0.8 GB at 5,000 subjects.
- `write_reference` takes the digests of the members' maps as an argument the contract lacks (`Reference` has no field for them) and `index.json` has a `schema_version`. A set reference whose id does not follow from its stage hash and those digests is refused on writing and on reading; a float64 map is refused, not cast; a stratum without members is left out on reading. `read_index` and `stratum_size` were added. Errors are `ValueError` and `FileNotFoundError`: the natural name, `ReferenceError`, is a Python builtin.
- The centroid of a cluster is the member with the largest projection on the z-weighted sum of the members' positions, which is the member nearest the normalized mean without a division. Sums over a cluster are accumulated in vertex order, so they do not depend on how numpy vectorizes.

Ceiling counts (`metrics/ceiling.py`).

- The two limits are compared in the type of the thickness array: the float32 nearest 4.9 counts as 4.9. A NaN is a vertex of the region and counts as nothing else. A margin as large as the maximum is refused. `thickness_above_max` is a function of its own.
- From the annotation alone only the search and the control region exist; `medial`, `inferior` and `lateral` need the template's cut. An annotation whose colour table lacks a parcel of the region gives no region, with a warning, because the remaining parcels would be another region under the same name.

Scores (`metrics/scoring.py`); see D27.

- `subject_metrics` takes `zmaps` (reference id to the subjects with a z-map against it), which the contract's signature lacked: without it a reference under which no subject has a cluster is not seen. Each per-hemisphere metric takes the larger hemisphere on its own. `composite` and `fallback_rank` always return the four `std_*` columns of the table; `fallback_rank` puts its percentiles there, so the tie rule of `add_rank` applies.
- A subject without a z-map has one row with the reference `none`, also in a run where others have a z-map, and is ranked among such rows.

Render schema 3 (`render/model.py`, `render/api.py`, `render/vertexmap.py`); see D24.

- `render.json` is refused by its own model when its three statements about counterparts disagree (`template`, `fsaverage_poles`, `vertex_map.fsaverage`). The `sphere.reg` files that a render with a template read are among its inputs whether or not they could be used, so a file that was repaired is seen as another input.
- `fill_fsaverage` sets a hemisphere that the mapping lacks to -1 throughout, also where the map held counterparts, so the result does not depend on what was filled before.
- `three_plane` refuses an output inside the fsaverage directory that the template was found in, as it refuses one inside the subject directory. When it looked for a template itself and found none, it says so once after the render; for a video alone it says nothing, since a video has no marks.
- The distance of the fsaverage pole is squared and summed in Python floats, so its digits do not depend on numpy's linear algebra.

The metric stages of a run (`metrics/pipeline.py`, `run.py`, `cli.py`).

- Surface holes in `scores.parquet` are those of the recon-all log. The contract has "`aseg.stats`, else the log" and leases `aseg.stats` in the `regional` stage; no table carries a per-subject value from that stage to the scores, and the two sources agree in all 34 real subjects. The `regional` stage therefore reads the two `aparc.stats` files only.

A hemisphere without a thickness file has no row in `vertex_summary`, whose vertex count is not nullable; the subject then gets the flag `no_thickness` in `scores`.
- A failed subject loses its rows in the part and its map, so that nothing of an earlier state passes for the present one. Two reasons exist beside those of `MetricError`: `source changed` and, for anything unexpected, `metric failure` with the type of the exception. The detail of an unreadable file names the file and the system's reason, not the path. The report lists a failure as `(unit, "<stage>: <reason>")`.
- Signatures of the stages on the run. `reference` is as the contract says, over the subjects with a current map. `score` is over the content of the three tables it reads (their SHA-256), the values it takes from the catalog and who is scored against which reference, where the contract had the regional signatures and the reference ids: the content says exactly when the scores would differ. The `reference` row names the reference's `index.json` as its output, which is where the later stages learn its id. The score stage refuses to run on a reference that is not current (`reference not current`), which only happens when it is named alone.
- A reference built within the run replaces the earlier one: other `set-` directories are removed.
- A shipped reference is applied when its mesh and its sphere digests equal the template's. Its components are standardised within the run like those of the set reference unless it carries percentiles for every component, in which case those are used. No shipped reference exists; the path is tested with a stand-in made from a set reference, and the percentile branch on scoring tables only.
- `Run.metrics(stages=None)`: without stages the four of `METRIC_STAGES` run, or with a dataset filter the two per-subject ones. It takes `retemplate` as well. `metrics_pending` was added, so that a source that is gone stops the call before anything is recorded, as for a render.
- `--fsaverage DIR` gives the template of one call and is not written to `config.yaml`. The run holds the template itself from its first use; a stored directory would be a path in the configuration (a census run has none) and would warn on every later call once the directory is gone. `render` takes `--fsaverage` as well, because a user who only inventories and renders would otherwise have no way to give one. A directory that was given and cannot be used stops the call when the run has no template to keep.
- `Run.template()` warns once per run object that there is no template, where it looked and how to give one. `Run.render()` says it at info level only: the long message on every render of a run without a template drowned the render's own lines, and `surfcensus metrics` is where it is said aloud.
- `Run.render()`, not `render/batch.py`, ends with the `native` stage, so the render package does not import the metrics. A render's row holds a template id only when a hemisphere was mapped. The `native` stage writes an empty table when no render has counterparts and removes the table when there is no cluster table.
- `Run.scores()` returns the primary rows, the subjects scored against a reference first, each group by rank. `Run.zmap` raises `KeyError` for a subject without a z-map.
- `status` prints one line for each metric stage that has run.
- Tests: `real_subject_fs7` gained the rest of Tier B (12 MB), so the FreeSurfer 7 subject is mapped and rendered with counterparts. The test of milestone 2 that the metrics raise was replaced. The tests pop `FREESURFER_HOME`, so an installed FreeSurfer cannot give them a template.

Added in the review of milestone 3 (robustness, 2026-11-13). Five findings on what a run does when files go away or its own outputs are damaged, each reproduced by a second check before anything was done. Four were changed and one is recorded as it is. Checked with tests on synthetic cohorts of 4 subjects and on the rendered run of 3 (`tests/test_pipeline.py`, `tests/test_templates.py`, `tests/test_store.py`, and for the exit codes and the printed lines `tests/test_cli_metrics.py`), each of which fails on a copy of the package with its fix undone, and by hand through the command line on a synthetic cohort of 5 subjects on fsaverage4, scored within the set, once when the fixes were made and once more when they were checked. No real subject was used for these.

- A subject that loses the files of a stage loses what the stage held of it. The units of `regional` are the present subjects with a stats file and those of `fsaverage` the ones with a thickness file, so a subject whose `stats/` folder or thickness files were removed after it was scored was no unit any more, and nothing replaced its rows: the scores kept its occipital-to-control ratio, or its ceiling fraction, without `no_stats` or `no_thickness`, computed from files that no longer existed. Each of the two stages now starts by removing, for the present subjects of its datasets that are not its units, their rows in the part of their dataset, their map, and their row in the ledger (`PublicCatalog.forget_work`, new). The merged tables change with that, their content is part of the score signature, and the scores are computed again. The ledger row has to go as well: files that come back with the listing they had (a folder moved away and back) would otherwise match a row that says `done` while the rows are gone, and `status` would count the subject as done. Outputs are removed first and the ledger row last, so an interrupted call is finished by the next one. By hand: with the stats folder of one subject and the thickness files of another moved away, `inventory` and `metrics` gave `2 done, 8 skipped`, the flags `no_stats,partial_score` with no ratio and `no_thickness,partial_score` with no ceiling fraction, and 4 units for each stage in `status`; with the files moved back, `4 done, 8 skipped` and the scores of before. Limit: the removal is part of the two per-subject stages. A call that names only `--stage score` or `--stage merge` does not do it, as it does not notice a changed file either.
- A template cache that does not read back is written again. `store_template` and `Run` wrote `templates/<template_id>/` only when `template.json` was absent. With a truncated `template.npz` the parent process built the template again from the fsaverage directory and went on, while every worker process read the damaged directory: each subject it worked on was recorded as `metric failure`, with `--force` all of them, and a failed subject loses its map. A fault of the run was recorded against its subjects (against D29), and stayed recorded after the cache was repaired. Now the directory is read back before it is used and written again when that fails or gives another template. Cost: one more reading of the cache per call, 5 to 10 ms for an fsaverage6 template of 5.9 MB once the file is in the page cache (the first reading in a process took 37 ms in one measurement and 0.33 s in another; measured on the cache made from the `fsaverage` next to `ds000102`, one machine). Skip, the refused `Enter` and the tour (asked for by Tomas on 2026-11-13 after a demo, where a bare `Enter` looked as if nothing happened and the app seemed to have one subject).

- The skip rule: a skipped subject is deferred, never dropped, never handed to another rater and never counted as a rating; every skip is logged; the rule is the same for a local rater, within the calibration stage and in a census. `k` (button `k skip`) stores nothing of the rating; the assignment stays open and comes back after every subject the rater has not been shown, the one skipped longest ago first, and a queue is done only when every subject has a submission. Reason: if a skip could move a subject to another rater or end a queue, which subjects a rater rates would depend on which they find hard, and those are the ones the census is about. The team branch of the queue is milestone 5; the rule for it is written in `app/queue.py` and has no code.
- Each skip is on record twice: `assignments.skipped_at` and `skip_count` (added without a new schema version, since no `app.sqlite` existed outside tests; `skip_count` is NOT NULL with default 0, where the request said nullable), and an event of type `skip` that the server writes and a page cannot post (`SERVER_EVENT_TYPES`), with the client time as `ts` and the server time, the number of the skip and the time on the subject in its payload. The assignment and the client time name a skip, so a request that arrives twice is one skip.
- `ms_on_subject` of a submission is the time of the visit that ended with it. The earlier visits of a skipped subject are in its skip events, and in the export as `behaviour.ms_skipped_visits` beside `n_skips`; the visit behind a rating that was revised is in `behaviour.ms_revised_visits` (science review, below). The export has a tenth table, `skips`, one row per skip, also for an assignment that was never rated: the contract's nine tables had no place for a skip of a subject without a rating, and the analysis reports skips per rater and per subject (`analysis_plan.md`).
- While a render of the selection is awaited, the queue answers `rendering` before a skipped subject comes back, which is the literal reading of "after every subject not yet shown". The price: in a run whose remaining renders never arrive, a skipped subject is reached only with `Backspace`.
- `Backspace` returns the assignment left last, rated or skipped. `k` on a subject shown again for revision sends nothing and leaves the earlier rating; without that the only way out of a revision was `Enter`, which stores a new revision.
- A refused `Enter` is answered beside the submit button: a box in a colour nothing else on the screen has, which names what is missing and the skip key, and the rows that lack something are framed. The button is no longer `disabled` while a rating is incomplete (`aria-disabled`), so a click on it is answered too.
- The tour: five steps, each beside the part of the screen it is about; it opens once per rater name and browser (a flag in local storage, set when it opens), again with `t` or from the help screen; its keys are read from the key map of the session; while it is open no key reaches the rating screen and playback is held; its time is not time on the subject; `tour_open` and `tour_close` are logged. It is a dialog for assistive technology by its roles and its focus handling, which was tested by assertions on the page and with no screen reader.

Marks (`app/marks.py`).

- The nearest vertex and its distance are computed in float64 from the positions of the vertex map; the KD-trees only find candidates, so equally near vertices go by rule (`lh` first, then the lowest native index) and the radius includes its boundary.
- Beyond the contract: `resolve_mark` validates the mark itself (`MarkError`) and raises `ValueError` for a snap radius above the render's `vertex_map_margin_mm` and for an index that cannot be that of the render; `check_snap_radius` is what the server calls at start, and a larger radius stops the server with a line that says how to lower it. `vertex_surface` is None whenever `fsavg6_vertex` is. Distances must be finite. `marked_vertices` returns int32.
- Checked 2026-11-13 on `ds000102` sub-01 with the fsaverage next to it, rendered at 320 px in the default and in the flipped orientation (`radiological` off, `sagittal_anterior_left` on): for a click at the centre of every opaque pial-coloured contour pixel (231,079 and 231,036 pixels, class A) the hemisphere, the pial and white vertex with their distances and the fsaverage6 vertex equal those found on the full surfaces and the two `sphere.reg` files; 13,800 further clicks per orientation (pial and white contour pixels and random places, all four classes) equal a search over every vertex, with voxel, tkr RAS and scanner RAS within 1e-9. The clicked points lie a median of 0.039 mm and at most 0.185 mm from the pial mesh, and nearer for the pixel-centre convention than for either corner (mean 0.042 against 0.094 mm). 0.305% of the contour clicks resolve to a vertex that is not `visible`; for 1.2% the white vertex across the midline is nearer than the one taken. One subject, FreeSurfer 6.0.1.

The database of the app (`app/db.py`, `app/models.py`).

- Every transaction of the engine is `BEGIN IMMEDIATE`, also one that only reads. With a deferred begin, two requests that read and then write were refused at once instead of waiting (the page sends events while a rating is on its way). The price: a second session opened while the first has a transaction open waits out the busy timeout (30 s local, 60 s team), so the app uses one session per request.
- The making of a new database is one transaction; a making that was killed leaves a file that holds nothing, which the next opening fills (checked with a child process killed after the tables were made). A file with the tables and without the `app_meta` rows is refused, not adopted.
- A partial unique index (`ux_submissions_current`) lets an assignment have at most one current submission. The write-ahead log of a team database is asked for after the checks, at every opening, so a local database that is refused in team mode keeps its journal mode. `app_db_mode` opens read-write without creating, because a read-only connection cannot roll back the journal of a killed writer (reproduced: `SQLITE_READONLY_ROLLBACK`).
- A rater's name that is empty or has white space at its ends is refused: `Ada ` beside `Ada` would be a second rater. Names are otherwise compared as given.
- Checked on Python 3.13.18 and 3.11.18 with SQLAlchemy 2.1.8, sqlmodel 0.0.49 and SQLite 3.53.1, one Linux machine, a local disk. Not checked: a network file system, macOS, Windows, two processes writing through the engine at once (the concurrency tests use threads).

The frontend (`app/static`, `app/templates`).

- A tile has one image element, made once and kept, and the viewer puts that element into the panel; the `src` of a shown image is never changed. So no tile is requested twice whatever the cache headers say. The price: the image elements of a panel are replaced at every frame.
- The panel size is set by a `ResizeObserver`, not by CSS alone: container-query units gave a height of 0 in Chromium 153 inside a flex column without a fixed height.
- No mark class is active when a subject opens; a click then places nothing. The playback mode goes back to synchronized with every subject; speed and zoom stay. `Esc` is a key of the page; `Enter` in the note field is a line break; the arrow keys pause a playing subject and then step.
- A click is taken at the middle of the screen pixel the browser names (`pointerAt`). The first version took the corner, as the formula `px = ox * width / w` reads with a whole-pixel offset, which put every mark half a screen pixel up and to the left on average: measured on four marks of the first drive on real tiles, the stored pixel lay 0.05 to 0.65 tile pixels left of and above the pixel centre that was aimed at (0.01 to 0.16 mm at a 454 px panel). After the change five marks at two window sizes, 2x zoom and a device pixel ratio of 2 lie within 0.5 tile pixels to either side.
- The draft of the open subject is kept in session storage, one draft per assignment, and an unsent rating is kept and posted again, also after a reload. The server therefore stores a rating that arrives twice (same assignment, client time and content) once.
- The review scatter plots the worse of the final A and B against the composite. Marks keep their size on screen under zoom.
- The review link of the rating page shows whenever the session is not blind; with a second rater the review is closed all the same until that rater is done, and the server then answers a browser that opens `/review` with a page that says so and links back (below), not with JSON.
- Checked in Chromium 153 only, headless; no Firefox, Safari, touch input or high-contrast mode.

The queue (`app/queue.py`), local branch.

- A subject is done by a completed `main` assignment of its present render, keyed by subject and render key. An open assignment whose render changed, failed or is awaited is left open and never returned again; the subject is offered anew under its present render. Without this, the 409 of a stale rating would send the page back to the same assignment for good.
- A `failed` render row of another config hash counts as out of date, not as failed: `surfcensus render` tries it again, so it is not a failure of the present configuration. Only a failure under the present hash makes a subject unratable.
- A repeat (off in local mode unless `app.repeat_rate` is set) is of the present render only and is drawn before the walk of the queue, so it can be the last assignment; a skipped repeat waits like any other subject.
- `subjects_left` and `subjects_skipped` are what the progress line shows; `progress.n_subjects` leaves out subjects whose render failed.

Final labels (`app/finalize.py`).

- The consensus of raters within one step is the lower value and the union of their marks without the marks of a criterion whose consensus is none; X marks stay. The contract has the plain union; a final mark of a class whose label is none would make vertices positive that the label calls clean. One rating follows the same rule (science review, below). Raters who all say unratable agree when they give the same reason.
- `FinalizeReport.unratable` counts failed renders that nobody rated; a subject rated before its render failed keeps its rating. `sync_unratable` also removes the label of a subject whose render is ready again.
- With several raters a submission removes the `single` rows that come from ratings and the consensus rows of its own subject when its ratings no longer give them, and writes nothing; `finalize` has to run again. A skipped assignment has no submission, so it is no rating.
- `freeze_calibration` leaves out subjects that were never rated, validates the set against the protocol before it writes, and writes the selection rows first and the file last.
- `surfcensus finalize` names each rater who still has subjects to rate, lists the open subjects by label with what each rater said, and can be run again.

The export (`export/tables.py`); see D28.

- Ten tables and a manifest; every table is built before anything is written, so a refused export leaves the directory as it was. The manifest has no time in it, so an unchanged run gives the same bytes. An export into a directory that holds an earlier one removes that manifest first and writes the new one last, and removes the table files of the other format that the earlier manifest lists; a file no manifest lists is left alone.
- The bin labels of the release are `1 to 9`, `10 to 29`, `30 to 99`, `100 or more`: a spreadsheet reads `1-9` as a date. `behaviour.n_frames_viewed` counts distinct frames per playback mode, not visits. `datasets.modal_field_strength` is the most frequent field strength of a dataset's subjects. `final_method` of a subject whose labels differ in method is the strongest of adjudicated, consensus, single.
- CSV has no types: a `subject_uid` that happens to consist of digits is read as a number unless identifiers are read as text. The README says so.
- A `back` from the screen that says nothing is left belongs to no assignment and is not logged, so `n_back` does not count it (seen in the drive on real data).

The server (`app/server.py`, `app/events.py`).

- One request is one transaction; a rating is not stored when its answer cannot be made, including the 503 for a next render that is recorded as done and cannot be read. The page keeps the rating and sends it again (seen in Chromium on a synthetic run).
- Beyond the `Host` check of the contract: a request for `/api`, or any POST, from another origin is refused (`Origin`, `Sec-Fetch-Site`); a POST is JSON of at most 16 MB; every answer carries a content security policy of `default-src 'self'` and forbids framing; no answer is a redirect (`redirect_slashes=False`, since `/media/catalog.sqlite` was answered with a 307). An integer of a body beyond SQLite's 64 bits is a 422 that names the field, found by fuzzing 4,500 bodies. uvicorn is pinned to `h11` and `asyncio`, so the served behaviour does not depend on whether `httptools` and `uvloop` are installed.
- A tile gets the immutable cache header only when it is asked for with the present render key; the contract sent it always. The cache of vertex indices is keyed by subject, render key and the stamp of the vertex map file.
- The server records `blind`, `require_marks` and the snap radius as `config_change` events when it starts and they differ from the last record, so the database says under which settings each submission was made. `sync_unratable` runs at start and whenever the failed renders changed.
- A closed unblinded route answers 403 with the `ErrorsOut` of every route. A request that asks for a page (`Accept: text/html`) gets the same words as a small page with a link back to the rating screen: a rater who clicks the review link too early should not see JSON. Added in the integration; the contract has JSON only.
- `serve` refuses a run whose renders are out of date and says so on stderr when selected subjects are not rendered yet: nothing renders behind `serve`, so the queue would wait for them for good.
- Team mode stops with a line that names milestone 5. The calibration branch of the server is written and only its examples are exercised.

`review` and the commands (`review.py`, `cli.py`); see D26 and D33.

- The app opens at the first render (D33, which replaced the context's default of rendering everything first). The first subject is rendered in the process itself, since starting a worker costs more than it saves. The rest is rendered by a second `Run` in a thread, in one call of `Run.render` with a batch size of `--jobs`, so each batch is recorded and offered when it is done. To end that call between two batches, `batches.run_batches`, `render.batch.run_render` and `Run.render` take a `stop` function that is asked before each batch; `Run.render` also takes `native=False`. Both are additions to modules of milestone 2. Chosen over many calls with a few subjects each: one call keeps its worker processes, and the `native` stage runs once at the end and not after every batch.
- Stopping: the server first, then the renderer, which finishes its batch (at most one render per worker, about 9 s on real data). The SIGTERM handler stays in place until the closing line is out. Before that, a second SIGTERM arriving while the command was stopping ended it with status 143 and without the line; seen when a wrapper passed the signal on.
- `review` returns the run in every case and has no error of its own for a run without a renderable subject; `review.rateable(run)` is 0 then and the command ends with 1. The contract says only that it exits 1.
- The default run directory is `PATH/../surfcensus-run` when a run is there or the place can be written to, else the current directory; for one subject directory as path it is beside the subjects directory. One subject directory as `PATH` on an existing run replaces the stored subject set: the path names it. A whole directory as `PATH` keeps a stored subject set, and the line that says how many subjects were found names the globs when there are any.
- `review` is for a local run with one source; a second local source cannot be added to a local run from the command line (`inventory --source` refuses another root), so the 34 real subjects of two datasets were reviewed as two runs.
- An `fsaverage` next to the subjects that cannot be used (not icosahedral, a missing file) stops `review` as it stops `metrics`, with the template's message and exit code 2. Not softened: a run that silently ignored a template it found would rank differently from what its user expects.
- `review --no-serve` and `export` need no package of the app. With the app, a core install is told to install the extra before any work is done.
- The summary lines name subjects by label; up to three incomplete subjects are named each with what they lack, more are grouped by what they lack.
- `surfcensus calibration freeze` takes `--version`. `surfcensus export --release` is offered and refused for a local run by the export itself.

Added in the review of milestone 4 (robustness, 2026-11-13). Five findings on what the app and the export do with an edited `protocol.yaml`, an output directory in a source and what a client sends, each reproduced by a second check before anything was done. Four were changed, one of them in part; one is recorded as it is.

- An `unratable` final label keeps its reason when `protocol.yaml` is edited. The label holds a position, and every reader looked that position up in the protocol the run has now. With the reasons put in another order (a valid protocol of the same version), `export` and the review screen renamed a subject that was rated `image too poor to judge` to `render failure`, beside a `ratings` table that still said the first; with a reason taken out, `GET /api/review` answered 500 and `export` stopped for the whole run. Now the database keeps the list that the positions refer to (`app_meta.unratable_reasons`, a JSON array; no column and no schema number changed). The three writers of `app/finalize.py` (`record_single`, `sync_unratable`, `finalize`) first bring that list and the labels up to the protocol they are given, by reason. A reason that the protocol has lost and that a label still names stays in the list behind the reasons of the protocol; it leaves with its last label and takes its place again when it is put back. The readers (`export`, the review screen through `subjects_frame`, `calibration freeze`) name a label through the kept list and no longer open the protocol for it. `finalize` moves nothing before it knows every label, so a `finalize` that is refused still changes no row.
- Chosen over what the second check had tried, which named a label by the reason text of the subject's current ratings when they agree: that leaves the label of a failed render, which has no rating, to its position, and a label that a later rating contradicts as well. With the list kept beside the labels a reader needs to know nothing of where a label came from. The price is a rule for whoever writes labels: positions are those of the kept list, so adjudication (milestone 5) has to call the same rebase, and a label written past it by another program is read with a list it was not written with. A database without the row (none exists outside tests) is read with the protocol of the run, as before. Added after the first CI run of the closed milestone 4 (2026-11-14). One cell of CI (macOS, Python 3.11) failed `test_the_app_of_a_review_stops_at_ctrl_c_and_at_sigterm` for SIGTERM on the commit after `03e29ad`: `review` had printed every closing line and then ended with return code -15, killed by the signal, where the test expects 0. The other three cells passed, and so had every earlier run.

- The cause. `review` set its SIGTERM handler when the app came up and put the earlier one back after the closing line; the command line had wrapped the call in a handler that raises `SystemExit(143)` and put the default back when the call returned. So between the closing line and the end of the process the default was in place again, and the second SIGTERM of the test, sent 50 ms after the first, killed the command whenever the stop had taken less than those 50 ms. D34 has "the SIGTERM handler stays in place until the closing line is out", which is what the code did and is not enough. `serve` had the same gap. Reproduced on Linux by sending a third SIGTERM when the closing line has been read: the command ended with -15 on the sources as they were.
- The change. The command line handles SIGTERM with one handler from the start of `review` or `serve` (`cli._StopSignals`): while `review` prepares, the first SIGTERM ends it with `SystemExit(143)`, as before; once the app runs, it sets an event that ends the serving in order; every later SIGTERM was meant to change nothing. After a SIGTERM, and after the app was served to its end, the handler is not taken away again, since the process is about to end. The review of the quick start (below, after D35) found two holes in this, both closed there: the handler set a `threading.Event`, whose lock a second SIGTERM could wait for for good, which hung `serve` and `review` (F1); and a handler written in Python does not last to the end of the process, because CPython puts the default back while the interpreter finalizes, so a SIGTERM 20 ms or more after the closing line still killed the command (F2). Since that review the process ignores SIGTERM from the first one on (`SIG_IGN`), and the stop is a flag without a lock (`stopping.py`). Added in the review of the quick start (2026-11-14). An independent review of the quick-start follow-up (D35 and the block after the first CI run of milestone 4) found seven problems, four major and three minor, each reproduced here before anything was changed, on light synthetic trees and runs. All seven were changed.

- F1, a burst of SIGTERMs hangs `serve` and `review`. The handler of the command line set a `threading.Event`, and `Event.set` takes the lock of the event's condition. A second SIGTERM that arrives while the first handler, or `Event.wait` in the main thread, holds that lock runs its handler on top and waits for the lock for good; `app.server.serve` had the same pattern (`lambda *_: waits.set()`). Reproduced with the reviewer's `hang.py`: `serve` on a rendered run hung at the first of up to 30 tries, five SIGTERMs at gaps of 0 to 50 ms once the app was up, the main thread three handler frames deep in `Condition.__enter__`. The change: a stop request is a plain attribute, `stopping.StopFlag`, whose `wait` polls with sleeps of 50 ms that a signal interrupts; the handlers of the command line, of `review.review` and of `app.server.serve` set such a flag and take no lock. `serve_review(stop=...)` and `serve(stop=...)` take a `StopRequest` (a `StopFlag`, or a `threading.Event` of a caller, which has the same two methods).
- F2, a SIGTERM after the closing line still killed the command. The block after the first CI run had the command line keep its handler to the end of the process; CPython puts `SIG_DFL` back for every handler written in Python while the interpreter finalizes, and leaves `SIG_IGN` in place (the reviewer's `fin.py`; `fin2.py` here, which ignores both signals and is not killed during a slow finalization). Reproduced with the reviewer's `late.py` (SIGTERM every 10 ms from the closing line on): `serve` ended with -15 in 6 of 6 runs, and `burst.py` in 11 of 12. The change: the first statement of the SIGTERM handler sets SIGTERM to `SIG_IGN`, so every later one is a no-op from then on; once the app was served to its end and the closing line is out, `CommandSignals.served` sets SIGINT to `SIG_IGN` as well. A second Ctrl-C while the app stops still ends the command with 130 and without the closing line.
- F3, mixed SIGTERM and SIGINT during the preparation gave tracebacks, exit code 1, hangs and workers left behind. The handler raised `SystemExit(143)` from inside the worker pool and the lock code (`batches._in_pool`, `concurrent.futures`), which are written for `KeyboardInterrupt`. Reproduced with the reviewer's `mix.py` (SIGTERM, then SIGINT twice, 1 to 3.5 s into `review --jobs 3` on a new run): 5 of 10 runs hung with `Exception ignored on threading shutdown ... KeyboardInterrupt` in `_python_exit`. The change, one piece of code for every command that had `_terminated_like_interrupted` (`render-subject`, `inventory`, `metrics`, `render`) and for `review` and `serve` (`stopping.command_signals`, `cli._stops_at_signals`): before the app is up, the SIGTERM handler marks that it was taken, sets SIGTERM and SIGINT to `SIG_IGN` and raises `KeyboardInterrupt`, the path that the batches take for Ctrl-C (running work is waited for, the leases are released, the interrupted batch is not recorded). The command prints `terminated; what was recorded stays, and the next call goes on` and ends with 143; Ctrl-C prints `interrupted; ...` and ends with 130. When Ctrl-C is already ending the command, a SIGTERM raises nothing and the code stays 130; a second Ctrl-C during the preparation is a person's way out as before. At the end of a command that a signal ended, both signals are ignored, so a late one cannot interrupt the finalization (where the hangs of `mix.py` were). When no signal was taken and nothing was served (a refused command, `--no-serve`, a caller in the same process), the earlier handlers are put back. A worker process gives SIGTERM its default in its initializer (`batches._start_worker`): spawned after the parent began to ignore the signal, it would otherwise inherit `SIG_IGN`, and the pool ends broken workers with SIGTERM. `render-subject` now ends at Ctrl-C with 130 and a line, where click's abort gave 1.
- Where the design departs from the one asked for: SIGINT is handled by a handler of `stopping` (it notes that Ctrl-C was taken and raises `KeyboardInterrupt`, as Python's does) wherever the process had Python's own handler, so that a later SIGTERM knows that Ctrl-C is ending the command; a SIGINT that the process ignores (a job of a shell without job control) stays ignored. Phase commands that end without a signal put the earlier handlers back as before, so a SIGTERM that comes after their work, while they print their report, kills them with -15, which is the default of any program.
- Not covered: a SIGTERM in the few instructions between the app coming up and the command noting it (`signals.serving = True`) still ends the command with 143 and the server and renderer threads with it, as the block after the first CI run said. A SIGTERM before the command has set its handler (while Python imports) ends the process with -15, before anything is written.
- F4, one default run directory for several sources. `default_run_dir` returned `<dataset>/../surfcensus-run` as soon as it held any `config.yaml`, before it looked for an earlier run of this root, so the run that `03e29ad` had made inside a dataset was not reached once another dataset of the folder had its run beside it; and the refusal `other root` said `--relocate if the data moved` also when the stored root still held its subjects, which, followed, rebound the other dataset's run (its renders and ratings) to this data under the same identifiers. - The -2 exits. Two kinds were seen. A signal that comes after the work, when the command has put the earlier handlers back and the interpreter is finalizing, ends it with -15 or -2, which is what any program does; two such runs were traced with `strace` (`rt_sigaction` of the main thread: the context put the handlers back, CPython set SIGINT to the default, the harness's SIGINT came 150 and 280 ms later). It is closed: `serve_review(stopped=...)` and `serve(stopped=...)` call the command line's `CommandSignals.served` once the app and the renderer have stopped and before the closing line is printed, and a test pins that order.

D36 (2026-11-14, Tomas). The functional viewer shows the raw and the preprocessed EPI series in order to make the effect of preprocessing visible, and is a quality-control tool for the raw data and for the preprocessing pipeline at once. His words:

The viewer is a prototype outside the package (`prototypes/func_viewer/`; its README is the hand-over). The decision is recorded here because it governs that work and because he made it the priority: read as, the viewer comes before milestone 5 until he says otherwise. It stands beside his decision of 2026-11-13 on the raw stream: "Raw bids means single per session registration to the anatomy to ensure comparability across sessions, processing stages, and subjects."

What follows, in the reading of the building session:

- The raw stream is the raw BIDS series, resampled once into fMRIPrep's T1w space through one stored rigid transform per session, that of the session's anchor run, and nothing else. No motion, slice-timing or distortion correction and no other processing is applied to it, also not to explain a difference between the streams.
- The preprocessed stream is the series as the pipeline wrote it. The pack records and the page states which steps the pipeline applied to the run (head-motion correction, distortion correction and its method, slice-timing correction), so that a difference between the streams is read as the effect of those steps.
- A difference between the streams, and the raw stream's misfit to the FreeSurfer contours, is the signal. It is not a confound, and cases are not selected against it.
- Both uses are named wherever a view is described: quality control of the raw data, and quality control of the preprocessing, of which the fit of the preprocessed series to the white and pial contours is one part.

What this corrected. An independent review of stage 1 (2026-11-14) found that fMRIPrep had applied susceptibility distortion correction in 5 of the 8 selected sessions, both quiet comparisons among them, read from fMRIPrep's own per-run reports, and that the raw and the preprocessed temporal means correlate at 0.85 to 0.95 there. The two runs without it and with little motion are at 0.98 and 0.9993; the two sessions without it and with much motion are at 0.85 to 0.95 as well, so the correction is one reason for a lower correlation and motion another, and how much each accounts for was not measured. The building session first reported this as something that contaminates the comparison and asked whether such sessions should be marked or kept apart. Under this decision it is one of the effects the comparison exists to show: the raw stream shows the distortion as acquired against the contours, and the preprocessed stream shows how far the correction brought the series onto the surfaces. The question was withdrawn.

What stage 1 holds (2026-11-13 and 2026-11-14; details, dates and material in the README of the folder). A complete listing of the functional runs of the 72 OpenNeuroDerivatives fMRIPrep derivatives made with fMRIPrep 23.0.2 or older: 9,373 runs have both streams, the transforms and the surfaces. A ranking of 9,186 of them by mean framewise displacement, read from every confounds table, and a selection of six high-motion and two quiet runs by a rule that was fixed in code before any table was read. Packs of the eight sessions and a browser page that plays both streams at up to 60 volumes per second, scrubs to the exact volume and through slices in three planes, and shows the difference, the stills and the contours. Under one registration per session, runs other than the anchor lie a mean of 0.5 to 6.3 mm from where their own registration would put them, and two runs are flagged at more than 10 mm; that is the head's movement between runs, shown as the raw data have it. The independent review recomputed the raw stream value by value with its own code, found no rigid misplacement in a shift search, and found the contours within 0.011 voxel of its own. One error was found and corrected before the review: raw series whose qform and sform differ were resolved differently by fMRIPrep in two datasets, so the raw affine is now the one that agrees with fMRIPrep's reference image of the run. No person has looked at a pack: every reading of a picture so far is an agent's reading of screenshots.

O30 (2026-11-14, Tomas; researched the same day, nothing built). A shared repository for what people find. His words: "it would be great to be able to create a way for people to inspect these data sets, and then for them to upload the results or their judgments and all of the visualizations to some repository that we create so can you research how this could be hosted and whether it's easy to implement that set of infrastructural elements into the package as it is now". Read as: a person runs the tool on a dataset, judges it, and sends the judgements, the tables and the renderings (surface tiles, EPI packs) to one place that the project runs, where others can see them. What the research found, from search results on the hosts' own pages (huggingface.co, help.zenodo.org and mriqc.readthedocs.io could not be opened from the building session, so every limit below is to verify):

- Precedent: MRIQC sends its image quality metrics and anonymized scan parameters to a public Web API unless `--no-sub` is given, stores a checksum of the image so that records of the same image can be matched, never the image, and takes expert ratings through a widget in its reports (Esteban et al. 2019, Scientific Data, doi 10.1038/s41597-019-0035-4). No precedent was found that also collects the visualizations.
- Three things of different weight travel: the judgements and tables (kilobytes per subject), the surface media (about 4 MB and 352 tile files per subject), and the EPI packs (about 0.5 byte per voxel, volume and stream: 34 MB for the worked example, gigabytes for a subject with many runs).
- Hosts. A Zenodo community: every contribution a record with a DOI, reviewed by curators before it joins the community; 50 GB and 100 files per record by default, so media go in as archives; an archive, not a place a web page can play tiles from. A Hugging Face dataset repository of an organisation: contributions as pull requests from anyone with an account, merged by maintainers; free public storage is "best-effort" with a request needed above about 1 TB, and recommendations of fewer than 100,000 files per repository; files are addressable by URL, so a static page can show them. An object store of our own (Cloudflare R2: no egress fee, 10 GB free, then a price per GB and month) behind a small ingest service: full control over validation, pseudonyms and removal, and someone has to run it. OSF (50 GB per public project, 5 GB per file; contributors must be project members) and GIN (git-annex; a limit of 50 GB per repository is reported by users) fit less well. OpenNeuro itself is excluded by the hard rule that nothing is written there.
- What the package has: `export` (ten tables and a manifest with the SHA-256 of every file), the media directory, the pack format, which is static files and one JSON document, the public side of the catalog (D20), and a release profile (D28). What it lacks: a bundle that holds tables and media together with a statement of what is in it, who made it and under which terms; a read-only form of the review screen and of the EPI page that reads files instead of the app's API (the `static-export` that `rater_app.md` plans); an explicit command that sends a bundle; and everything on the receiving side (validation, removal, merging the judgements of several people on one subject).
- What stands in the way is not code. (1) Identity: a local run names its subjects under a salt of its own (D21), so two people who judged the same public subject cannot be matched. Matching needs either the dataset named in the open (accession and subject label), or an unkeyed digest of the source files, which anyone can compute from the public files and D20 forbids for that reason; D10's pseudonyms survive only if a service of ours receives the names and publishes pseudonyms. (2) O28: nothing functional of a public dataset is published by this code, and the EPI interface records no judgements (O27). (3) Data that are not public: renderings of a participant's brain are personal data, so a contribution of one's own study needs that study's consent and ethics to allow it; OpenNeuro data are CC0 and structurally defaced, so renderings of them may be redistributed. (4) What the collection can be used for: contributions are a convenience sample by self-selected raters, some with a score-ordered queue, so they can feed a gallery, training material and the detectability analysis with care, and not the prevalence estimate, which needs the sampling of phase 5.

Default in force: none of it is built; nothing leaves a user's machine. Proposed order if he wants it: first the bundle and the static viewer in the package, which are useful without any host (a bundle beside a paper, a bundle sent to a colleague); then a pilot repository restricted to public datasets, surface interface only, opt-in by an explicit command; the choice between naming datasets and D10, and O27 and O28, decided before anything of it is public.
