# Quantitative metrics

Cortical thickness is the quantitative anchor: a number per subject and per location that can always be returned to when choosing which brains to look at and when testing what the raters saw. Outlier detection is done primarily in fsaverage space, where every subject's thickness is compared with the distribution at the same anatomical location across many subjects.

## The failure mode and what it should do to thickness

When the pial surface absorbs the superior sagittal sinus, the transverse sinus or the tentorium, it moves outward past the true gray/CSF boundary on the cortical faces adjacent to those structures. The white surface is usually unaffected; sometimes it is also pulled when bright non-brain tissue gets labelled as white matter. The expected signature is local:

- inflated thickness in contiguous patches, not across a whole parcel;
- on the medial occipital and medial parietal surface next to the falx and superior sagittal sinus;
- on the inferior occipital and posterior inferior temporal surface over the tentorium and transverse sinus;
- often near or at the thickness maximum, because capture can add several millimetres.

This is a hypothesis about the signature, derived from where the structures sit. The pilot tests it against rated and click-marked examples before scores are trusted.

## Why fsaverage space

Normal thickness varies strongly across cortex, and occipital cortex in particular varies over short distances (thin calcarine cortex next to thicker surrounding cortex). A patch of 3.5 mm cortex is unremarkable in one place and extreme in another. Parcel means dilute local patches, and per-subject native ROIs cannot say what is normal at a given location. Resampling every subject to a common mesh gives a per-vertex normative distribution, so each subject's thickness becomes a z-map. It also places rater click marks (see `rater_app.md`) on the same vertices, so detection can be evaluated spatially, not only per subject.

## Resampling to fsaverage

Inputs per subject: `surf/?h.thickness` and `surf/?h.sphere.reg` (about 0.6 and 5.3 MB per hemisphere; both present in OpenNeuroDerivatives subjects, verified on `ds000102` sub-01), and `label/?h.aparc.annot` when the subject has it.

Target mesh: fsaverage6 by default (40,962 vertices per hemisphere), with fsaverage (163,842) as an option (`metrics.mesh`).

### The template

Everything the metrics need on the target mesh is derived at run time from an `fsaverage` directory (`templates.py`, decision D22); nothing fsaverage-derived ships with the package. The directory is looked for in this order:

1. the directory given with `--fsaverage DIR`;
2. `$FREESURFER_HOME/subjects/fsaverage` if FreeSurfer is installed;
3. the `fsaverage/` directory next to the subjects (fMRIPrep writes one into `sourcedata/freesurfer/`): of the first dataset, in pseudonym order, that has one with every template file. It is looked for when the template is first needed, where the inventory recorded it and otherwise at its usual place, so a directory that was put there after the inventory is found without a second inventory.

A directory can be used when it holds `surf/?h.sphere.reg`, `surf/?h.white`, `label/?h.cortex.label` and `label/?h.aparc.annot`; `surf/?h.white.avg.area.mgh` is used when both are there. nilearn's `fetch_surf_fsaverage` also returns sphere meshes, but whether its coordinates match FreeSurfer's `sphere.reg` convention is to verify, and it is not used.

What is derived, per hemisphere:

- Vertices: the first 40,962 of the full mesh. Icosahedral meshes nest in their vertices: `fsaverage5/surf/lh.sphere.reg` equals the first 10,242 vertices of the fsaverage `lh.sphere.reg` exactly (2026-11-12). Every per-vertex file (white surface, annotation, average area) is cut to that length, and the cortex mask is the vertices that `cortex.label` lists below that count.
- Faces: the faces of a coarser mesh are not a subset of the full faces. They are derived by coarsening, one icosahedral order at a time: every vertex that the coarser mesh lacks has exactly two neighbours in it, its parents, and each face made of three such vertices gives one coarse face of the three parents they share. Applied to the real fsaverage this gives 81,920 faces for fsaverage6, and applied once more it gives the faces of `fsaverage5/surf/lh.sphere.reg` exactly (checked 2026-11-13).
- Regions, all inside the cortex mask, from the Desikan parcels of `aparc.annot` (`metrics.regions`). The search region is cuneus, lateraloccipital, lingual and pericalcarine, plus the part of precuneus and fusiform whose white-surface y lies at most 45 mm in front of the occipital pole (the cortex vertex with the smallest white y; vertex 12950 on `lh`). Its medial part is cuneus, pericalcarine and the precuneus part; its inferior part is lingual and the fusiform part; the lateral part is the rest, which is lateraloccipital. The control region is caudalmiddlefrontal, rostralmiddlefrontal and superiorfrontal. On the real fsaverage6 (`lh`, `rh`): cortex 37,487 and 37,482 vertices; search 4,856 and 4,715; medial 1,851 and 1,874; inferior 1,405 and 1,351; lateral 1,600 and 1,490; control 5,789 and 5,804.
- Vertex areas. The fsaverage white surface is an average of coordinates and is smaller than real cortex by a factor that differs between regions: against FreeSurfer's own average of subject vertex areas (`white.avg.area.mgh`, 82,220 mm2 on `lh`) the fsaverage6 white-mesh area is 0.77 over the cortex, 0.59 in lateraloccipital, 0.68 in cuneus, 0.72 in lingual and 1.01 in insula (measured 2026-11-13). So when the average-area files are there, the area of a target vertex is its own average area plus half the area of every vertex that was added between it and a neighbour, order by order; the total is the file's. A rule that gives each dropped vertex whole to the nearer target vertex decides by a difference in distance of half a percent and leaves areas between 0.21 and 7.65 mm2; with halves they range from 0.66 to 5.59 mm2. Without the files the area is a third of the incident faces of the white mesh, and the template says which it used (`area_source`).

A template is identified by the content of the files it was read from (`template_id`, 12 hex characters, also over the mesh name and the region settings), and stored in the run directory under `templates/<template_id>/` (5.9 MB). Worker processes read it from there, so a directory that does not read back as the template (a truncated `template.npz`, a damaged `template.json`) is written again from the template that the run found, before any subject is worked on. A run keeps the template it first used: the id is pinned in the catalog, and a run that has scores never falls back to the ranking of D16 because a volume was unmounted. `--retemplate` takes another one, and every map is then made again.

Two copies of fsaverage were compared (2026-11-13): the one next to the subjects of `ds000102` (FreeSurfer 6.0.1) and the one next to those of `ds003020` (7.3.2). `?h.sphere.reg`, `?h.cortex.label`, `?h.aparc.annot` and `?h.white.avg.area.mgh` are byte-identical. `?h.white` differs in its creation line (2013 and 2018) and in the position of one vertex, number 40969 in both hemispheres, by 0.07 and 0.11 mm. That vertex is not on fsaverage6, so the two templates hold the same arrays under two ids (`b710aa6e0467` and `8da171b90692`).

### The mapping

Both spheres are put on the unit sphere. For each target vertex a KD-tree gives the 8 nearest subject vertices (`metrics.knn`); the faces around them are tested for containment, nearest vertex first, and the first face that contains the point gives its barycentric weights. A point that no such face contains takes the value of the nearest vertex (a fallback). A sphere whose faces are wound inward is reversed once. This is pure numpy and scipy, written without BLAS so that the result does not depend on the linear-algebra library. FreeSurfer's `mri_surf2surf` uses a different default mapping (to verify which), so values will differ slightly; the comparison on one subject waits for a reference array made with FreeSurfer (`tests/test_network_metrics.py`).

Measured on `ds000102` sub-01 (2026-11-13): 3 (`lh`) and 2 (`rh`) of the 40,962 targets fall back with 8 neighbours and none with 16; an exhaustive search over all faces agrees with the map on 2,000 random targets per hemisphere; no face of the subject's or of fsaverage's `sphere.reg` is wound inward; one map takes 0.4 s.

What a fallback is, and what it costs. A fallback says that none of the faces that were searched contains the point, not that no face does: where the sphere is sheared or folded, the face of a point touches none of its 8 nearest vertices. Measured on the 68 hemispheres of the 34 subjects below (2026-11-13): 103 of the 2.8 million targets fall back, in 43 hemispheres, all of them of FreeSurfer 6.0.1 subjects. With 16 neighbours 22 still do, with 32 neighbours 8, with 64 neighbours 4 and with 256 none, so every one of them lies in a face. The nearest corner of that face is the 9th to 16th nearest vertex for 81 of them, and up to the 204th in `ds000102` sub-04 `rh`, which has 26 of the fallbacks and a `sphere.reg` with 12,768 of its 297,584 faces wound inward. 66 of the 103 targets lie in the template cortex. At the 65 of them that have a value either way, the thickness of the nearest vertex differs from the value in the face by 0.33 mm in the median, by more than 0.5 mm at 24 and by more than 1 mm at 9, at most by 2.03 mm (0.91 mm outside that one hemisphere). So a fallback can put an error of the size of an outlier on a single vertex of a map. In this set none reached a metric: 3 of the targets lie in the search region and none in the control region, and at those three z is 0.7, −1.1 and −2.6 with the nearest vertex and about 0.3, −0.1 and −1.6 with the face. `vertex_summary.parquet` holds the number of fallbacks per hemisphere (`n_fallback`), not which vertices they are.

The setting decides how far the search goes: a larger `metrics.knn` in `config.yaml` finds more of the faces, gives the targets that 8 neighbours resolve the same face, and has every map made again. Tried with 32 on a copy of that run: 8 fallbacks were left (7 of them in the one hemisphere), 58 stored values differed, the clusters were the same 1,132 with the same vertex counts and peak vertices, and no composite and no rank changed. One map took 0.37 s with 8 neighbours, 0.42 s with 16, 0.55 s with 32 and 0.82 s with 64. The default stays at 8 until it is decided whether leftover targets get a wider search (open question O19). Nearest-vertex correspondences, which the vertex maps of renders and the native positions of cluster centroids use, do not depend on the setting.

Missing values. A subject vertex counts when its thickness is finite and above 0 and, with an annotation, when the annotation calls it cortex (a parcel other than `unknown` and `corpuscallosum`). A target vertex whose corners that count carry less than half of the weight has no value (`metrics.min_valid_weight`); otherwise the remaining weights are renormalized. Target vertices outside fsaverage's `label/?h.cortex.label` have no value. So zeros and the medial wall do not leak into the map.

A hemisphere gets no map, and a flag in `vertex_summary.parquet`, when its `sphere.reg` is missing (`no_sphere_reg`), when thickness, sphere and annotation differ in length (`vertex_count_mismatch`), when the sphere is degenerate or more than 1% of the targets fell back (`degenerate_sphere`), and when fewer than 90% of the template's cortex vertices get a value (`low_coverage`). The subject is still scored on what it has. It fails only when a file that is there cannot be read.

Storage: per subject, two float16 arrays (one per hemisphere) of fsaverage6 thickness, 164 kB, in `tables/fsavg/<subject_uid>.npz`; a hemisphere without a map is NaN throughout. 5,000 subjects take under 1 GB. float16 changes a thickness by at most 0.002 mm. Everything downstream (global median, reference, z, clusters) is computed from the stored maps, so it can be reproduced from the run directory alone. z-maps are not stored; `Run.zmap(subject_uid)` computes one from the map and the reference (decision D25).

Caveat: FreeSurfer's spherical registration is driven by folding patterns (curvature and sulcal depth), so a misplaced pial surface should affect registration little. A misplaced white surface could. Subjects rated major on the white-surface criterion are analysed separately.

## Per-vertex scoring

Global normalization: subtract each subject's median cortical thickness over the whole hemisphere outside the occipital search region (template cortex vertices with a value). This removes offsets from age, scanner and contrast that shift thickness everywhere. A hemisphere with a value at fewer than 2.5% of the mesh's vertices outside the search region has no global median.

Reference distribution at each vertex: median and MAD of normalized thickness across reference subjects in a stratum. A stratum is a set of subjects that share a reference, and it exists when it has at least 30 members with a map (`metrics.min_reference_n`): the subjects of one dataset (`dataset:<pseudonym>`); else those of one field-strength bin (the nearest of 1.5, 3 and 7 T) and one FreeSurfer major version (`field:3|fs:7`); else all subjects (`all`). A subject is scored against the first of these that exists, and skips the middle one when its field strength or version is not known. The reference of a stratum is built from every subject that fits its definition, whether or not that subject is scored against it: in a census of three datasets of 40, 40 and 10 subjects at 3 T, the 10 are scored against a field-and-version reference built from all 90. The reference includes the subject being scored. A local run knows a field strength only when it is given (`--field-strength`).

A vertex needs values from at least 20 subjects (`metrics.min_vertex_n`), else it has no reference. The MAD map is smoothed on the mesh, twice replaced by the mean over each vertex and its neighbours, and floored at 0.05 mm.

Subject z-map: z_v = (t_v − global median − median_v) / (1.4826 × MAD_v).

How often z exceeds 3 by chance depends on the size of the reference. Simulated with independent Gaussian noise at every vertex, each subject scored against a reference that includes it and with the smoothed MAD: 0.0030 of the vertices at 15 subjects, 0.0025 at 20, 0.0021 at 30, 0.0017 at 60 and 0.0015 at 200, against 0.00135 for a normal distribution (2026-11-13). Real maps are smooth, so their chance clusters are larger than those of the simulation (one to three vertices).

Circularity: if capture is common at some vertices, it shifts that vertex's median up and inflates its MAD, which hides exactly the errors we look for in exactly the place they occur. The first pass uses all subjects; after rating, the reference is rebuilt from rater-passed subjects only, and all scores are recomputed. The paper reports detection with both references, since the difference measures how much contamination of the reference costs.

A reference built within a run is stored under `tables/reference/<reference_id>/`: `index.json`, one `stratum_<nnn>.npz` per stratum (median, MAD and subject count per vertex, 0.98 MB on fsaverage6) and `members.parquet`. Its id (`set-<12 hex>`) follows from the settings and from the digests of the members' map files, so a reference whose member changed has another id. Before the `reference` stage is skipped, the reference is read back; one that does not read as a reference of its id on the template's mesh is built again.

## Subject-level metrics derived from the z-map

- Outlier clusters: connected components on the fsaverage mesh of vertices with z above a threshold (default 3, `metrics.cluster_z`), positive side only. They are found separately in each of the five regions (search, medial, inferior, lateral, control) and per hemisphere, so a cluster that crosses the border between the medial and the inferior part is one cluster in the search region and one in each part, clipped. The five largest per region and hemisphere are kept in `clusters.parquet`.
- Order: by area, then by excess mass, then by lowest vertex number. Rank 1 is "the largest cluster".
- Area: the sum of the template's vertex areas over the members, which are average subject areas where the template has them (see The template), so clusters in different parcels are ranked on one scale.
- Mass: `mass` is the sum of z over the members and `excess_mass` the sum of z minus the threshold. Noise makes clusters in every subject (see the null rate above), and `mass` jumps from 0 to at least 3 when one vertex crosses the threshold, while `excess_mass` starts at 0. The composite uses the excess mass (`scoring.mass`). Neither is weighted by area, and the number of vertices is stored beside them.
- Peak z and its vertex, and the centroid: the member nearest, on the sphere, to the z-weighted mean position of the members.
- Medial and inferior split: the same cluster metrics restricted to the medial and inferior parts of the search region, defined on fsaverage by parcel. This replaces per-subject direction-of-normal ROIs, which are no longer needed for scoring.
- Control: the same metrics in the frontal control region, to separate subjects with occipital-specific outliers from subjects with noisy surfaces everywhere.

Each cluster centroid maps back to the subject's native surface through the vertex map of the subject's render (`clusters_native.parquet`, decision D25): the candidates are the native vertices whose nearest template vertex is the centroid, or one of its mesh neighbours when there is none, and the one nearest their mean pial position is taken, with its pial position in tkr RAS. This is a nearest-vertex mapping, not an interpolated point, and it needs no source file, so it can be made after the Tier C files are gone. A centroid outside what the tiles show, which is the normal case for the control region, has no native position. The renderer can then centre an optional view on it, and the review screen can show it.

## The 5 mm ceiling

`mris_thickness` documents a default maximum thickness of 5 mm. How vertices exceeding it are stored (clamped to 5 mm, or set to 0) is not stated on that page (to verify in source and empirically). Clamping produces a pile-up at 5.0 that is itself a sign of capture. Zeroing would make captured cortex look thin and would also corrupt interpolation on fsaverage. In that case zero-valued vertices inside the cortex label must be treated as missing and counted separately. On `ds000102` sub-01 (FreeSurfer 6.0.1) values are clamped at exactly 5.0 and in-cortex zeros are negligible (5 vertices; see `CLAUDE.md`). The source agrees (read 2026-11-12 at v6.0.0, v7.0.0 and v7.4.1): `MRISmeasureCorticalThickness` measures the distance from white to pial and from pial to white, clamps each at the maximum, and stores their mean; the only zero it writes is for ripped vertices. Two consequences. A vertex reads exactly 5.0 only when both directions exceed the maximum; when one does and the other measures d, the stored value is (5 + d) / 2, so a threshold at 4.9 undercounts vertices whose search was truncated. And the function prints the two truncation counts ("thickness calculation complete, N:M truncations"): a candidate Tier A metric. On `ds000102` sub-01 (FreeSurfer 6.0.1 under fMRIPrep) the line is in the per-hemisphere logs, once in `scripts/recon-all-lh.log` (39:225) and once in `scripts/recon-all-rh.log` (93:434), and not in `recon-all.log` (checked 2026-11-12); the file roles `recon_log_lh` and `recon_log_rh` name those logs. The same holds for one FreeSurfer 7.3.2 subject (`ds003020` sub-UTS01: 22:230 and 23:325). The inventory stores the two counts per hemisphere, in the order of the log (`trunc_lh_1`, `trunc_lh_2`, `trunc_rh_1`, `trunc_rh_2`); which of the two is the white-to-pial search has not been established. Where a plain `recon-all -all` run writes the line has not been checked; the inventory assumes two lines in `recon-all.log`, left hemisphere first. In that FreeSurfer 7.3.2 subject the maximum of `lh.thickness` and of `rh.thickness` is exactly 5.0 as well (2026-11-12), so over-maximum thickness is clamped there too. Phase 0 still inspects thickness histograms of known-bad subjects before this is treated as settled. Either way, the ceiling fraction (fraction of native search-region vertices at or above 4.9 mm, or at exactly 0) is reported as its own metric.

How the fraction is counted (`metrics/ceiling.py`), on the subject's own vertices, before any resampling: an interpolated value at the ceiling would need every corner at the ceiling. With a map, a native vertex belongs to the search region when its nearest template vertex does and, when there is an annotation, the annotation calls it cortex (region kind `template`; 21,484 `lh` vertices of `ds000102` sub-01, none of them at 4.9 mm or more). Without a map the region is the subject's own occipital parcels (kind `annot`; 16,429 vertices on that hemisphere), which lacks the precuneus and fusiform parts. The count on the subject's own parcels is stored for every hemisphere that has an annotation (`ceiling_frac_annot`), so subjects with and without a map can be compared on one definition. The control region is counted the same way.

What the fraction cannot see: the stored value is the mean of two clamped distances, so a vertex reads 5.0 only when both searches were cut short. With one cut short and the other measuring d it reads (5 + d) / 2, which is below 4.9 whenever d < 4.8. The count at 4.9 mm or more is therefore a lower bound on the vertices with a truncated search. Its companion is the pair of truncation counts from the logs, for the whole hemisphere: `scores.trunc_frac` is their sum over twice the number of native vertices. A hemisphere whose largest thickness exceeds the maximum by more than 0.01 mm was measured with another maximum and gets the flag `thickness_above_max`.

## Regional metrics (Tier A, every subject, no surfaces needed)

From `?h.aparc.stats`: `ThickAvg` and `ThickStd` for the occipital parcels and a frontal control set. These are cheap and available even when surfaces are missing, so they serve as a fallback score and as a baseline in the detectability analysis. They show how much the fsaverage z-maps add over what anyone can compute from stats files.

The occipital set is cuneus, lateraloccipital, lingual and pericalcarine; the control set is caudalmiddlefrontal, rostralmiddlefrontal and superiorfrontal (`RegionConfig.occipital`, `RegionConfig.control`). A region's thickness is the mean of `ThickAvg` over its parcels, weighted by `SurfArea`, with both hemispheres pooled; parcels that a file lacks are left out, and a subject with one stats file is scored on that hemisphere: the `regional` stage takes every subject with at least one of the two files, where `tier_a` of the inventory means both. The ratio is occipital over control. Measured 2026-11-13: 0.811 on `ds000102` sub-01 (1.947 and 2.402 mm) and 0.828 on `ds003020` sub-UTS01 (2.112 and 2.551 mm). `mris_anatomical_stats` writes no row for a parcel without vertices (read in source at v6.0.0 and v7.4.1), so a file can hold fewer than 34 parcels. A parcel whose thickness is written as `nan` gets no row in `regional.parquet`, with a warning; no such file has been seen.

Surface holes, as a general quality covariate: `scores.holes_lh` and `scores.holes_rh` are those of the recon-all log (`orig.nofix lhholes = N, rhholes = M`). `aseg.stats` carries the same counts (`lhSurfaceHoles`, `rhSurfaceHoles`) when `mri_segstats` was called with `--euler`, as `1 - euler / 2` (read in source at v5.3.0, v6.0.0 and v7.4.1), so the Euler number of the surface before topology fixing is `2 - 2 * holes` and is not stored. The two sources agree in all 34 subjects of the measurement below; the metrics read the log's only. Qoala-T found surface holes to be the most important single predictor of manual include/exclude ratings (see `prior_art.md`).

## Intensity metric (Tier C, rendered subjects)

Sample `T1.mgz` along each native vertex's line from white to pial at a few fractional depths. Dura and sinus differ from gray matter on T1, but the sign depends on sequence, flow and contrast agent. So the test is an outlier test against the subject's own control-region profile: the fraction of search-region vertices whose outer-depth intensity falls outside the control region's 1st to 99th percentile. Resampled to fsaverage like thickness, it can join the z-map as a second channel.

Not built yet: it is computed inside phase 6, while the T1 and the surfaces of a rendered subject are on disk (milestone 5).

## Composite score

The maximum over a small set of standardised subject-level metrics (`scoring.components`): the excess mass of the largest cluster in the medial and in the inferior part of the search region, the ceiling fraction, and the regional occipital-to-control ratio. Every component is oriented so that larger is more suspicious, and per-hemisphere metrics take the larger of the two hemispheres. The maximum suits a failure that can appear in one place only. The composite is a ranking device for selection and review, not a classifier. Its predictive value is estimated against ratings in `analysis_plan.md` and reported, never assumed.

Scale. Each component becomes rank-based normal scores, norm.ppf((r − 0.5) / n), with r the rank of the value among the n subjects that have the component; tied values share the lowest rank of the tie. Cluster mass and ceiling fraction are 0 for most subjects, and a robust z fails on such metrics: 26 zeros and the values 10, 50, 200, 1000 have MAD 0 and IQR 0, the scale becomes the standard deviation, and a cluster of mass 50 scores 0.27. Normal scores give −2.13 for the zeros and 1.19, 1.38, 1.64, 2.13. The composite is therefore a rank statistic, not an effect size: the largest value of a component gets the same score whether it stands far out or not. `scoring.standardize = "robust_z"` keeps the other scale.

Population. Components are standardised over the whole run (`scoring.population = "run"`), not within the dataset: ceiling fraction and the regional ratio are raw numbers that compare across datasets, and standardising them inside each dataset would remove the differences between datasets that the census is about. The cluster masses carry the stratification through the reference. A cluster mass is standardised among the subjects scored against the same reference; every other component among all subjects of the run that have it, each counted once, so a subject without a z-map is scored on its ceiling fraction and ratio against everyone. `"stratum"` standardises within the subject's stratum; a subject without one is scored among all.

Partial scores. A subject that lacks a component (no `sphere.reg`, no stats files, low coverage) has a maximum over fewer scores, which is lower on average. `n_components` says how many it had and the flag `partial_score` marks it. The flags `no_stats` and `no_thickness` say what the score lacks, not why: a subject whose `regional` or `fsaverage` stage failed on a file that is there (a truncated thickness file, say) gets them like a subject without the files, and the reason is in `surfcensus status --failures`. Rank: within one reference, rank 1 is the highest composite; ties go by the mean of the subject's standardised components, then by `subject_uid`; subjects without a composite come last.

`scores.parquet` has one row per present subject and reference. A subject without a z-map has a row with the reference `none`. Exactly one row per subject is primary, and `Run.scores()` returns those.

`rank` orders the rows of one reference, so it is not one order of the subjects: a subject without a z-map is ranked among the rows of `none`, and in a run where the others have z-maps it is rank 1 there, beside the rank 1 of the scored subjects. `primary_rank` is the one order: it numbers the primary rows from 1 to the number of subjects, those scored against a reference first, by rank, then those without one, by rank, and equal ranks under two references by `subject_uid`. It is missing in the rows that are not primary. `Run.scores()` is sorted by it, and the rating queue and the deciles of the selection take it, not `rank`. In a local run `Run.scores()` also names the subjects (`subject_label`); the table itself holds no label.

## Local users

The shipped normative reference (see `architecture.md`) is a set of per-vertex median, MAD and subject-count maps on fsaverage6 per stratum, 0.98 MB per stratum. It is empty today (decision O7). When there is one, local subjects are resampled and z-scored against the stratum of it that fits them (field strength and FreeSurfer version, else version, else all), provided it was made on the same fsaverage spheres as the run's template, and those scores are the primary ones of a local run. When a local set has at least 30 subjects with a map, a within-set z-map is also computed. With no applicable shipped reference and fewer than 30 subjects, no z-map is computed and subjects are ranked on raw ceiling fraction and occipital-to-control ratio (decision D16).

The D16 ranking gives each subject, per component, the percentile (r − 1) / (n − 1) with r the minimum rank among the n subjects that have the component, and the larger of the two is its score. When no subject has a vertex at the ceiling every ceiling percentile is 0, so the order is that of the ratio. The maps are still made when there is a template, so the set can be scored the moment it reaches 30 subjects.

The same ranking is used when no fsaverage template is found. `surfcensus metrics` then says where it looked and how to give one (`--fsaverage DIR`).

## Measured on 34 unrated subjects (2026-11-13)

Inventory and metrics were run on the Tier A and Tier B files of every subject of `ds000102` (26 subjects, FreeSurfer 6.0.1) and of `ds003020` (8 subjects, 7.3.2) from OpenNeuroDerivatives, as two local fMRIPrep trees in one run, with the `fsaverage` next to them. No field strength was given and neither dataset has 30 subjects, so all 34 are in one stratum (`all`) and each is scored against a reference that includes it and mixes the two FreeSurfer versions. None of these subjects is rated. The numbers say how the metrics behave; they say nothing about which subject has a surface error.

- Resampling. Every hemisphere got a map. Fallbacks per hemisphere: 0 in 25 of the 68, 1 to 7 in 42, and 26 in one (0.06% of the targets); each of the 103 lies in a face that a search over more neighbours finds (The mapping). At least 99.5% of the template's cortex vertices got a value in every hemisphere (median 99.85%). The global median is 2.35 to 2.66 mm (median 2.49) in the FreeSurfer 6 subjects and 2.41 to 2.61 mm (median 2.55) in the FreeSurfer 7 subjects.
- Check against the stats files. The mean of the map over the template vertices of a parcel, weighted by vertex area, was compared with `ThickAvg` of that parcel in `?h.aparc.stats`, for all 34 parcels of all 68 hemispheres (2,312 pairs): correlation 0.983; difference (map minus stats) median 0.009 mm, quartiles −0.013 and 0.037 mm; absolute difference median 0.024 mm, largest 0.52 mm (precentral, one hemisphere). - Composite. With normal scores every component has the same spread by construction, and the largest value of each gets 2.18 with 34 subjects. Three subjects share that score: one has the largest ceiling fraction and the largest ratio, one the largest medial mass, one the largest inferior mass. The component that decides the composite is the ratio for 10 subjects and each of the other three for 8. For the ceiling fraction this means: the 22 subjects without a vertex at the ceiling or at zero score −2.18 on it, and the 12 with one to three such vertices score 0.42 to 2.18, in the order of the fraction. One subject is at rank 4 through three vertices, two at 4.9 mm or more and one at zero. So in a set like this one, where almost nothing is at the ceiling, the component orders subjects by single vertices and has the same say as the others (open question O17).

## What not to do

- No fixed universal thresholds in the paper's main analysis. Thresholds are for cluster definition, selection and display, and the analysis reports sensitivity to them.
- Do not train a classifier on the census ratings and then report its accuracy on the same ratings. Anything fitted is evaluated by leave-one-dataset-out cross-validation.
