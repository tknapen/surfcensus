quality control of the raw data and of the preprocessing

Quality control of the raw data: motion, artefacts in single volumes, distortion and dropout as acquired, movement between runs. Quality control of the preprocessing: what was corrected, what remains, how the preprocessed series fits the white and pial contours.

What this page shows

Every functional run of a subject, session after session, as one continuous moving picture in the anatomy. The page serves two kinds of quality control at once: of the raw data, and of the preprocessing. Two streams of every run lie on the same slices, with the white and pial surfaces of FreeSurfer as contours:

  • raw: the raw BIDS series, resampled once (cubic interpolation) onto the grid of the preprocessed series by the session's one rigid placement (the stored registration of the session's anchor run), and nothing else. No motion, slice-timing or distortion correction, and no other processing. That one resampling smooths single-volume detail slightly, and it enters the difference. The raw stream shows head motion within a run, artefacts confined to single volumes, distortion and dropout as acquired, and how far the head moved between runs.
  • preprocessed: the series as the pipeline wrote it, voxel for voxel on its own grid. It shows what remains after the pipeline's corrections, and how the corrected series fits the contours.

The difference between the two streams, and the raw stream's misfit to the contours, are what the page exists to show. The strip under the timeline says what the pipeline did to the run on screen, each statement with where it was read; details gives the whole text.

One stream. The runs follow each other without a stop: playing runs on from the last volume of a run to the first of the next, and from the last run of a session to the first of the next session (sessions put in order by: ). At a boundary nothing changes but the pictures: the slices stay at the same place in the anatomy (on a run with another voxel grid, the slices nearest to that place), the panels keep their place and size, and the view, the layers and the rate stay. The voxel grid moves between sessions, since each session has its own one placement. Boundaries are marked on the timeline and in the traces: a dashed line between runs, a thick solid line between sessions; the current volume is the magenta cursor. A run on the timeline jumps to its first volume.

A run that is not the anchor is drawn through the anchor's registration, so the raw stream shows how far the head moved since the anchor run. A run that its own registration would put more than mm elsewhere is marked flagged: that is a statement about the raw data under one registration, not a fault of the page.

Intensity normalization (n), off by default. Off: one linear window for the whole stream, so that a run that is brighter or darker than another is seen as it is. On: each run under its own window. The legend under the controls says which is on and what the gray levels stand for. The difference is taken of the gray levels on screen, and its scale is given in gray levels and in units of signal under either setting.

Exact values. Every voxel on screen is one block of a stored 8-bit value (through the table of the common window when normalization is off), no smoothing. The orientation letters lie in a margin outside the picture.

Markers. A volume whose framewise displacement exceeds the threshold has a band in the traces, and the frame carries a red marker that stays for at least ms. Volumes that the confounds table marks as non-steady-state have an amber band and marker.

Playing paints every volume; when the computer is slower than the rate, playing takes longer and nothing is skipped. The stream is held in memory in pieces around the current volume, up to for the whole stream.
